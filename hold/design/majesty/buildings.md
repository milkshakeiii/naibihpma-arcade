# Majesty Gold: buildings, economy and the sovereign's tools

Scope: Majesty: The Fantasy Kingdom Sim (Cyberlore, 2000) plus The Northern Expansion (together "Majesty Gold"). Unmarked claims come from the official Majesty Gold HD manual (Steam PDF). `(fan)` marks claims from the fan wiki, Steven Carter's quest guide or the gameplay.tips guide. `(unsure)` marks claims I could not confirm, or where sources disagree. Gold figures marked (fan) are the fan wiki's and may be from one patch only.

The one idea behind the whole game: you cannot give orders to heroes. You build places, pay for things and put bounties on targets, and the heroes decide for themselves where to go. Every building is a reason for a hero to go somewhere and spend money. Every hero class has its own idea of which reasons count.

---

## 0. How the economy hangs together

- **Start of a quest.** You begin with a treasury and a Palace (sometimes an Outpost instead). Building, recruiting, bounties and sovereign spells are all paid for out of that treasury.
- **Where gold comes from.** Heroes earn gold from monsters, chests, lairs and flags. They carry it and spend it at shops, at the guild or temple they recruited from, and at vices. Buildings hold a coffer of gold, and Tax Collectors carry it to the Palace. Marketplaces make the most. Everything else makes a trickle or nothing.
- **Guilds and temples take a cut.** Heroes eventually return part of their money to their home guild or temple, where it waits in the coffer. The Rogues Guild returns a smaller percentage than the others.
- **The Palace has a ceiling.** The manual says there is a limit to how much gold the Palace can generate each economic cycle. It also says buildings "without cost multipliers" no longer add to the Palace's automatic income.
- **Flags and recruits are the main sinks.** A bounty is paid once, and a removed flag's gold is lost. Recruiting and spells are paid each time.
- **Repeat builds cost more.** The first building of a type is cheapest and each duplicate costs more. Wizards Towers are an exception (the manual says so). Per the fan wiki, so are Ballista Towers and Royal Gardens, which are "spammable" (fan).
- **Blacksmith discount.** One Blacksmith lowers the cost of all new construction. More Blacksmiths do not add to it. The fan wiki gives about 5% (fan).
- **Library discount.** One Library lowers research costs slightly, and a second adds nothing (manual). The fan wiki gives 5% (fan).
- **Money that never comes back.** Elven Lounges and Gambling Halls pay no taxes. What heroes lose there is gone from your economy.

### Tax collection
- **Collectors and routes.** Tax Collectors are henchmen. They leave the Palace (or a Guard House), walk to each building on the Tax Route, then return and deposit the gold in the treasury. Every building that can hold gold has a Tax Route toggle and a Tax Once button.
- **New buildings default onto the route.** The manual warns you to notice this. Manage the route by hand once you have houses and outlying buildings.
- **Minimum Pickup.** This is set on the collector. A building whose coffer is below the threshold is skipped. Carter's guide recommends raising it to something like 500 in big cities so collectors do not waste walks on houses and inns while marketplaces overflow (fan).
- **Minimum Return.** The collector keeps collecting until he carries this much, and only then heads home. He ignores the limit and goes home when there is nothing left to collect.
- **More collectors.** The base number equals Palace level, up to three. The manual says every five Fairgrounds, Blacksmiths, Royal Gardens and Marketplaces make a Fountain appear, and each Fountain gives one extra collector. (The reference section lists "inns, blacksmiths, gardens and marketplaces" instead of Fairgrounds. This is a manual inconsistency, so treat the exact list as unsure. Carter says cheap Inns can produce an early Fountain (fan).)
- **Guard Houses as banks.** A collector can drop off at a Guard House, and the gold is credited at once. Collectors working from a Guard House cut the walk back to the Palace. Outposts also work as drop-off points.
- **Dying with the money.** A collector killed on his rounds drops what he carries. If a hero is nearby, the hero picks it up. If no hero is near, the gold is lost. Ratmen from Sewers specifically ambush collectors. Carter says to put Guard Houses beside Sewers to protect Peasants and collectors (fan).
- **Healers follow collectors.** The wiki's Healer behaviour list gives a 50% chance of following and healing a Tax Collector (fan).
- **Extortion.** With a Rogues Guild you press Extort. Every building's taxes arrive in your treasury at once and the Rogues keep a sizeable cut. Level 2 keeps a smaller share. The manual calls it an emergency measure. A gameplay.tips author suggests firing Market Day just before an Extortion, so the market's fresh pile is included (fan).

### Peasants, building and repair
- **Who builds.** Peasants come from the Palace and assemble buildings and upgrades. More workers means faster work. Gnomes (and Dwarves) also help. Wizards Guilds, Wizards Towers and the Sorcerers Abode are built by magic and need no Peasants.
- **Placement.** You see an outline with a buffer circle. Red means the spot is illegal. Some buildings need to be a minimum distance apart (Marketplaces and Trading Posts) or within range of another structure (Wizards Towers). You also cannot build in the dark of the fog, so heroes have to explore first. Cost is deducted when you place the order.
- **Repair.** Peasants repair damaged buildings. Each building has Repair Queue (always repair) and Repair Once. You can only repair a building before it reaches zero hit points. A destroyed building must be replaced. Only fully repaired buildings can research or upgrade. Peasants can misjudge priorities in a crisis, so the manual suggests taking minor buildings off the repair queue.
- **Demolishing.** You get back a small share of the build cost.
- **Peasant refuge.** Peasants can shelter in Guard Houses, so they do not walk all the way back to the Palace.
- **Buildings you don't place.** Houses, Elven Lounges, Gambling Halls, Fountains, Gazebos, Sewers and Graveyards appear on their own. Sewers and Graveyards cannot be destroyed (the Northern Expansion's Broken Sewer Main and Ancient Graveyard can). The Palace-region Houses cannot be demolished according to the manual (it lists houses with the self-generated structures that "cannot be demolished or destroyed", unclear for every case, so unsure).

## 1. Palace

**What it does.** The Palace is the center of the kingdom, and losing it ends the quest. It places Reward Flags, opens the build list and the roster, and supports your henchmen: Peasants, Tax Collectors and Palace Guards.

| Level | Henchmen | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 Peasants, 1 Tax Collector | Gnome Hovels, Rangers, Rogues and Warriors Guilds, Blacksmith, Guard House, Inn, Marketplace, and Trading Posts once a Marketplace exists |
| 2 | 4 Peasants, 2 Collectors, 1 Palace Guard | Wizards Guild, Statues; Dwarven Settlements, Elven Bungalows, Ballista Towers, Libraries, Wizards Towers (each with its own prerequisites); Temples to Agrela, Dauros, Fervus, Krolm and Krypta |
| 3 | 6 Peasants, 3 Collectors, 2 Palace Guards | Temples to Helia and Lunord; Royal Gardens; Fairgrounds |

- **Upgrade requirements.** Level 2 needs four living heroes and Level 3 needs twelve, plus gold. A hero's gravestone doesn't count.
- **Upgrade cost.** The wiki has about 3,000 and 3,750 gold for the two upgrades (fan, unsure).
- **Gold.** The Palace pays no taxes the way a shop does. It produces some income, capped per cycle (see above). A higher Palace level raises Marketplace income (see Marketplace).
- **Related buildings.** In the Northern Expansion, a level 2 Palace also unlocks Outposts, Embassies and Magic Bazaars. A level 3 Palace unlocks the Hall of Champions.
- **Interactions.** Rogues can be bribed to attack another player's Palace in multiplayer. Rogues are famed for disloyalty. Paladins are almost unbuyable.
- **Players say.** Get the Palace to level 2 early. It unlocks Wizards and temples, so players recruit a fourth hero quickly. Carter's guide tells you to train a cheap Rogue just to reach the number.

## 2. Guilds you recruit from

All guilds and temples work the same way. You press Recruit, pay gold, and a timer runs. The hero arrives and begins adventuring at once. Each building supports a fixed number. The Heroes button lists members, and Visitors lists passers-by.

- **If a guild is destroyed.** Its heroes walk home to the Palace and leave. If you build a replacement before they arrive, it adopts them.
- **If a hero dies.** A gravestone stays for a limited time (longer for higher levels). Resurrection or Re-animate on the gravestone brings the hero back. The gravestone holds a guild slot. Recruiting a replacement while all slots are used dismisses the lowest-level gravestone.
- **Hero levels.** Heroes gain levels by adventuring. More hit points come with each level, and some gain spells or skills.

### 2.1 Warriors Guild
- **Does.** Four slots, shared by Warriors, Warriors of Discord and Paladins. The building is sturdy (fan: about 700 to 800 hit points).
- **Requires.** Nothing, so it is available from the start. Warriors of Discord need a Temple to Fervus. Paladins need a Temple to Dauros. You cannot have both temples, so you pick one (per the Krolm and Fervus restrictions below). Recruit costs (fan): Warrior 450, Warrior of Discord 900, Paladin 1,000.
- **Guild skill: Call to Arms** (500 gold, fan). It teleports every resident to the guild's entrance. Guides use it to "pull" warriors to a marketplace under attack, or to move a strike force before using Gate.
- **Gold.** The coffer is fed by hero tithes and is on the tax route.
- **Hero behaviour.** Warriors hunt monsters. They buy gear at the Blacksmith. Per the wiki, Warriors and Blacksmiths "go together like bread and butter" (fan). Warriors of Discord have very low intelligence and rarely buy potions. A Library raises intelligence for newly recruited heroes, which makes them more likely to buy (fan). Paladins are smart enough to buy and use healing potions.
- **Healers and Warriors.** Healers follow Warriors 75% of the time, and Warriors in turn protect healers (fan).
- **Players say.** Players use Warriors Guilds as the wall of the town because of their high hit points. Carter says to put them on the perimeter and use Call to Arms if nobody answers an attack (fan). A Warriors Guild beside a marketplace or trading post also draws fights toward it (fan).

### 2.2 Rangers Guild
- **Does.** Four Rangers. Rangers explore by default. Once the map is fully explored they sometimes leave for distant lands and return later if they survive (manual). The fan wiki says they may return with better levels and gear (fan).
- **Guild skill: Move Camp** (800 gold, fan). It moves the whole guild, even while it is being attacked. Carter says that if a lonely starting Rangers Guild is going to be destroyed anyway, it costs less to let it fall and rebuild (fan).
- **Hero behaviour.** Rangers' sight range and attack range grow with level. A Ranger can stand twice as far from a kill and still share the loot (fan). They will not go after monsters they cannot hurt: the manual says a Ranger will not attack a Skeleton however big the bounty.
- **Herbs and potions.** Rangers make healing potions from herbs (fan). Healers plant the healing plants (their behaviour list gives "Plant healing plants" 50%) (fan). Rangers also use poison from a level 2 Rogues Guild (fan).
- **Players say.** Because they start exploring on their own, the manual's "Sovereign's Tip" recommends a Rangers Guild as your first building. Barbarians have ties to Rangers (in-game text). Carter pairs them (fan).

### 2.3 Rogues Guild
- **Does.** Four Rogues. The guild returns a smaller share of income than others.
- **Level 1.** It has the **Extort** skill (see Tax collection above). It also causes a **Gambling Hall** to appear if you have Elves.
- **Level 2.** Heroes with high artifice can pay to poison their weapons. Poisoned targets lose hit points over time and can die from it. It makes Extortion cheaper (fan).
- **Hero behaviour.** Rogues have the highest artifice, high luck, low willpower, and are obsessed with finding and stealing gold. The manual calls their "usual activity" stealing. They fight with crossbows and avoid chain and plate. They are the cheapest hero (fan: 275 gold).
- **Poison harvest.** The manual says Cultists sow poisonous plants that Rogues later harvest to poison their weapons.
- **Loyalty.** A Rogue's only loyalty is gold, and Rogues respond to bounties cheaply. The fan wiki says they will scout your borders for a small fraction of what other heroes demand (fan). In multiplayer they may answer a flag placed on your own Palace.
- **Stealing.** Rogues can steal. In "Urban Renewal", Red Rogues "might steal from your buildings" (fan). Cultists also occasionally steal from local businesses (official site text on the wiki) and have a small chance of stealing gold from monster lairs (fan).
- **Players say.** A level 2 guild is mainly about cheaper Extortion. A low-level Rogue is the usual tool for demolishing an Elven Lounge (fan). In The Siege, Rogues and Rangers chase enemy caravans (fan).

### 2.4 Wizards Guild
- **Requires.** Palace level 2. Built by magic.
- **Does.** Four Wizards. Each level gives two sovereign spells, and all of them are range-limited to the guild or a Wizards Tower. Extra wizard towers extend the net.
- **Level 1.** Farseeing and Invisibility. Heroes may pay to have gear enchanted to +1. Allows Wizards Towers and Libraries.
- **Level 2.** Lightning Bolt and Anti-Magic Shield. Enchants to +2. Allows you to Enchant Wizards Towers.
- **Level 3.** Lightning Storm and Supercharge. Enchants to +3.
- **Costs per cast (fan).** Farseeing 200. Invisibility 400. Lightning Bolt 400. Anti-Magic Shield 500. Lightning Storm 1,600. Supercharge 500. Guild cost about 1,500 and 2,500 for the level 2 upgrade, with each additional guild costing more (fan).
- **Wizard behaviour.** Wizards "research", and they are cautious. They have very low hit points (the wiki says second lowest at level 1). The wiki's decision list for Wizards gives these percentages (fan):
  - Learn a new spell at the Library 90%
  - Train intelligence at the Library 55%
  - Train magic resistance 15%
  - Follow and support a Barbarian 30%, or a Monk 20%
  - Raid a lair 45%
  - Raid an enemy building 30%
  - Fight wandering monsters 65%
  - Explore 50%
  - Visit Royal Gardens 5%
  - Defend the Library and Marketplace if under assault
- **Players say.** Wizards need Healing Potions and a tank. Cast Invisibility on a low-level wizard so it can gain experience safely (fan). Vampires reflect spells and can kill Wizards by reflecting their own spells back. Rock Golems and Medusae resist magic. Wizards make a poor core army on maps with many Medusae or Dryads (fan).
- **Gold.** Enchants are paid by heroes and credited to the guild coffer.

### 2.5 Wizards Tower
- **Requires.** A Wizards Guild, and it must be placed within range of the guild or of another tower.
- **Does.** Extends the area where Wizards Guild sovereign spells can be cast. Enchanting a tower (needs a level 2 guild; fan cost 400) makes it fire energy bolts at enemies for a limited time, then you can do it again.
- **Gold.** No coffer. Cost for the first one is about 500 (fan). The manual says the cost does not climb like other buildings.
- **Interactions.** In multiplayer, towers see another player at war as hostile. The Supercharge spell doubles the casting range of towers and guilds.

### 2.6 Library
- **Requires.** A Wizards Guild. All level 1 research must be done before upgrading. Costs (fan): about 600, then 800.
- **Passive.** Lowers research costs (see above). Newly recruited heroes have higher intelligence from a Library. Fan wiki: more libraries raise that further (the stated formula of 2^(x-1) libraries per point is unsure).
- **Research, level 1 (fan costs).**
  - Fire Blast, 500. Teaches Wizards at level 3. It hits several targets and has a long recharge.
  - Train Intelligence, 250. Wizards go and study.
  - Magic Resistance, 250. All heroes may study to raise resistance (manual). The wiki adds that it gives Wizards a Magic Resistance spell at level 6 (fan).
- **Research, level 2 (fan).** Meteor Storm 1,500, which is the big Wizard spell for level 7 Wizards. Power Shock 500 and Flame Shield 500, which "intelligent heroes" other than Wizards (Monks, Priestesses) can learn.
- **Hero behaviour.** This is the building behind your designer's "Wizards learn new spells at the Library as they level". Wizards visit when idle (90% in the wiki's list), and the wiki says non-wizards go "when there is nothing better to do". The manual's own hint is that Wizards who mill about want a Library. If you have not researched a spell, nothing happens.
- **Interactions.** Heroes defend the Library if it is attacked. Wizards guard it with their own lists (fan).
- **Players say.** Fire Blast is the worthwhile one. Meteor Storm is not cost effective (fan). Only Wizards use Meteor Storm.

### 2.7 Temples (shared rules)
- **Exclusions.** Temples are tied to religion and some cannot coexist:
  - Agrela, Dauros, Fervus and Krypta: Agrela and Dauros are exclusive with Fervus and Krypta. Agrela and Dauros can coexist with each other.
  - Krolm cannot coexist with any other temple.
  - Helia and Lunord exclude each other and Krolm. Both are available on top of the main paths.
- **Palace level.** Agrela, Dauros, Fervus, Krypta, Krolm need level 2. Helia and Lunord need level 3.
- **Sovereign spells.** Temple spells have no range limit. You can cast them on anything you can see. Each cast costs gold.
- **Spell icon colours.** Wizards purple, Agrela aqua, Dauros gray, Krypta red, Fervus green, Helia yellow, Lunord brown.
- **The 5 build paths (gameplay.tips, fan).** Summoning (Krypta, Fervus, Helia or Lunord), Righteousness (Dauros, Agrela, Helia or Lunord), Barbarian (Krolm alone).

**Temple to Agrela** (Healers, four).
- Spells: Healing (L1), Blessing (L2), Resurrection (L3).
- Heroes: Healers heal others (95%), follow and heal Warriors (75%), Wizards (35%) and Tax Collectors (50%), plant healing plants (50%), visit the Inn (20%) and Royal Gardens (50%), and train magic resistance (35%) (fan). They get free self-resurrection by level (fan). They may follow and heal enemy kingdoms' units in multiplayer. Statues and Royal Gardens cut that down (fan).
- Carter says to get Agrela early on caravan quests, because caravans can be healed like any unit (fan).

**Temple to Dauros** (Monks, four).
- Spells: Stone Skin (L1), Vigilance (L2), Petrify (L3). Fan costs 200, 800, 1,500.
- Unlocks Paladins at the Warriors Guild. Monks have very high willpower and almost never visit Gambling Halls or Elven Lounges. The official site text says a Dauros temple makes marketplaces more profitable (fan, from official text on the wiki).
- Mausoleum: heroes of Agrela and Dauros do not use it.

**Temple to Krypta** (Priestesses, four).
- Spells: Wither (L1), Animate Bones (L2), Re-animate (L3). Fan costs 300, 800, 2,000.
- Priestesses drain life and raise skeletons, and at higher levels they control the undead. Animate Bones used on a neutral corpse can raise it, so target carefully (fan). Re-animate revives with part of the hit points (manual: "some", fan: about half).
- Heroes can learn Power Shock and Flame Shield at the Library (fan).

**Temple to Fervus** (Cultists, four). Level 1 gives Healing and raises the luck of heroes you recruit afterwards. Level 2 Illusionary Hero. Level 3 Vines. Unlocks Warriors of Discord. Cultists charm and shapeshift into animals and sow poisonous plants for Rogues. The fan wiki says the temple can spawn animals to charm (fan, unsure).

**Temple to Krolm** (Barbarians, six in the Northern Expansion, four in the original per the fan wiki). Provides Rage of Krolm, which temporarily boosts every hero (fan cost 1,500). The manual text I extracted also lists Vigilance and Petrify near the Krolm entry, which looks like a page-layout mix-up with Dauros (unsure). Barbarians rarely buy from shops, which saves you from supporting a Marketplace and Blacksmith (fan). They are strong on offense and poor at defense. Rage is for rush attacks.

**Temple to Helia** (Solarii, four). Fire Strike (L1, which does not harm buildings) and Sun Scorch (L2). Solarii defend Guard Houses and have area fire. Costs 350 and 1,200 (fan). Guard Houses give Solarii refuge.

**Temple to Lunord** (Adepts, four). Winged Feet (L1) and Wind Storm (L2). Fan costs 400 and 1,000. Adepts patrol the whole settlement and run fast. Winged Feet is useful for chasing caravans.

## 3. Commerce

### 3.1 Marketplace
- **Does.** The best income building. Its coffer fills by itself and is taxed. Research adds items for heroes.
- **Requires.** Nothing (Palace level 1). It must be placed some minimum distance from other Marketplaces and from Trading Posts.
- **Research.**
  - Level 1: Healing Potions (fan 300 gold) and Market Day.
  - Level 2: Rings of Protection (fan 750), which add to armor.
  - Level 3: Amulets of Teleportation (fan 1,000). Heroes use them to flee or return home.
- **Income.** It rises with level and with Palace level (manual, "greater amount of gold"). Fan formula: base 125 to 250 depending on market and Palace level, plus 3 per House, 5 per other Marketplace and 5 per Trading Post, doubled by an Elven Bungalow. Carter's rule of thumb: about 250 per game day at level 1 and about 50 more per upgrade, so several level 1 markets beat one level 3 market (fan, unsure about both).
- **Market Day.** A button that creates a large pile of gold in the coffer at once. The Marketplace then closes for a while. It sells nothing and earns nothing, and caravans stop coming. Fan sources say it takes about 45 seconds and pays about double normal, and that it throws heroes out of the shop, which can pull heroes off shopping and back into fights (fan). Elves already double income, so the wiki says not to bother with Market Day with them (fan).
- **Heroes at the Marketplace.** Heroes buy potions, rings and amulets, subject to intelligence. A potion costs the hero about 50 and puts about 20 in the coffer (fan). Wizards, Healers and Elves like potions, and Rangers use them too. Barbarians and Warriors of Discord rarely do.
- **Elves.** The manual lists "Performing at Inns and Marketplaces" as the Elf's usual activity.
- **Dangers.** Trolls love attacking Marketplaces and caravans (manual). Ratmen from Sewers (and Minotaurs) raid them too. Sewers pop up next to buildings, and the manual says to surround the Marketplace with other buildings so a Sewer cannot spawn adjacent. Put a Guard House with Arrows beside each Marketplace, so monsters have another target and Ratmen are drawn off (manual and fan).
- **Players say.** Keep it by the Palace entrance, close to the tax route. Upgrade it and your Palace for more gold (fan). Protect it first, because losing it early often loses the quest (fan). Level 3 matters most where caravans arrive, since the destination's level multiplies the caravan's cargo (fan).

### 3.2 Trading Post
- **Requires.** A Marketplace. Must be a minimum distance from every Marketplace and other Trading Post.
- **Does.** Now and then sends a Caravan to the nearest Marketplace, which drops its cargo as gold into the Marketplace coffer. The farther the Trading Post is from the market, the more gold the caravan carries. Level 1 can also research Healing Potions for sale.
- **Interactions.** Trolls and other monsters ambush caravans and attack the post. Ogres often spawn in front of Trading Posts and go for them (fan). The manual says to put them on the frontier and say distance equals profit, and warns that a long route is a tempting target. Fans put a Guard House or Ballista Tower or a Warriors Guild beside each post (fan).
- **Players say.** Carter says the cargo is multiplied by the destination Marketplace's level (fan). Caravans do not spawn during a Market Day (fan).
- **Quests.** "Trade Routes" requires getting about half of 30 caravans to your single Marketplace over 31 days. Random bad events strike every few days (fan). In "The Siege", the enemy caravans funding Borjin are what you can destroy (fan).

### 3.3 Blacksmith
- **Does.** Lets heroes buy better weapons and armor, and lowers building costs.
- **Research.** Weapons and Armor, level 1 +1, level 2 +2, level 3 +3. All research at a level must be done before the upgrade. Fan research costs 200, 300 and 400.
- **What heroes do.** They visit to upgrade. Fan figures: weapons about 100 to 300 gold per tier for the hero, armor about 300 to 900. The Blacksmith keeps about half and the other half goes to the coffer (fan, unsure).
- **Who ignores it.** Wizards and other robed casters skip armor, unarmored classes buy weapons only. Barbarians prefer to spend at the Blacksmith over the Marketplace (fan). Healers buy only weapons (fan).
- **Extra.** Dwarves receive a random +4 item from destroying a building while wearing +3 gear (fan).
- **Prerequisite for.** The Dwarven Settlement needs a level 3 Blacksmith (so it is a gate for the Dwarf path).
- **Players say.** Upgrade when heroes are high level. Warriors plus Blacksmith is the best pair. Get one early for the construction discount (fan).

### 3.4 Inn
- **Does.** A place where heroes can rest and recover away from their guild. Put Inns on the frontier. They produce a small income.
- **Heroes.** Resting heroes pay a small fee (fan about 4 gold per rest). Elves play music here and earn gold. They have no coffer, so the money is for their own shopping (fan). Healers visit at 20% (fan).
- **Interactions.** It is a prerequisite for Elven Bungalows and counts for Fountains per Carter. Carter says one Inn makes about 100 per game day and the cost climbs slowly, so half a dozen Inns pay back within a week (fan).
- **Gazebo.** A free Inn that appears once you plant Royal Gardens.
- **Players say.** The manual's tip says Inns and Trading Posts need to be built on the frontier so heroes can recover without walking home. Carter says Inns are an overlooked money building (fan), and one quest guide places Inns to protect the edges of the Palace area (fan).

### 3.5 Guard House (and Ballista Tower)
- **Does.** Supports one City Guard who patrols the area and who is replaced for free when killed. It is a drop-off for Tax Collectors and a shelter for Peasants and Solarii.
- **Research.** Arrows (needed for level 2) make it fire automatically. Level 2 can research the Veteran Guard.
- **Hit points.** Low for the cost (fan about 200 and 275). Cost climbs per copy.
- **Interactions.** It is bait: monsters go for the nearest building, so it protects the Marketplace. Elf heroes run from Guard Houses with Arrows, so a ring of them keeps enemy elves and rogues off (fan, Carter). Carter says Guard Houses are poor perimeter defense, and best inside the town, beside Marketplaces and Sewers (fan).
- **Ballista Tower.** Dwarf-only, very expensive, high defensive value, and its cost does not climb (fan). The Dwarven Settlement itself, and the Fairgrounds (manual lists "Fires bolts at nearby enemies" under the Dwarven Settlement entry) shoot bolts. The Fairgrounds entry in the manual is garbled in the extracted text, so I would not rely on it (unsure).

### 3.6 Fairgrounds
- **Requires.** Palace level 3. The manual's entry "Defense / Fires bolts" appears to be a text-extraction mix-up with the Dwarven Settlement; the Fairgrounds' main function is training.
- **Does.** Heroes can buy combat skill training and take part in tournaments. You research Tournaments, then pick the type: Hand to Hand, Archery, Magic, or Combo. You can also pick No Contest, which closes the tournaments and pushes heroes back out to the field.
- **Heroes.** Matching classes arrive to practice. Melee tournaments take Barbarians and Warriors. Archery takes Rangers and Cultists. Magic and Combo take nearly everyone (fan). Good performers get experience and sometimes bonus gold.
- **Gold.** It is taxable and counts toward Fountains. Fan wiki says it is a good tax earner and gives approximate costs of 3,000, 9,000 and 27,000 (fan, unsure).
- **Danger.** In Slay the Mighty Dragon Carter takes the Fairgrounds off the tax route while Vendral is approaching so collectors do not die (fan).
- **Players say.** Wizards and Paladins are leveled at the Fairgrounds, which is how late-game armies are built without risk (fan).

### 3.7 Royal Gardens
- **Requires.** Palace level 3.
- **Does.** Heroes visit to relax and meditate, then leave with a random beneficial spell effect. It produces gold (taxed) and raises hero loyalty in multiplayer, so they are less likely to answer an opponent's flags or use the opponent's buildings. Creates a free Gazebo.
- **Heroes.** Healers visit 50% of the time and Wizards 5% (fan).
- **Players say.** Cost does not climb, so it is spammable (fan).

### 3.8 Statues
- **Requires.** Palace level 2.
- **Does.** Cosmetic, plus loyalty in multiplayer. Fan: 5% loyalty each, base cost 600, 60 hit points. Useful only in multiplayer or in quests with a rival kingdom (fan).

## 4. Non-human races (pick one)

You can have only one of Gnomes, Elves or Dwarves.

### 4.1 Gnome Hovel
- **Requires.** Nothing, but no Elves or Dwarves. Three Gnomes per hovel. After the first is built others appear by themselves, up to three.
- **Does.** Gnomes are poor fighters but fast, cheap builders and repairers. New heroes get slightly more luck. If all their homes burn, the Gnomes leave.
- **Players say.** Get them early for a quick town. They stink (the advisor's dialogue in the manual). They fix buildings fast after dragon attacks, though dragons attack repairers (fan).

### 4.2 Dwarven Settlement
- **Requires.** Palace level 2 and a level 3 Blacksmith. No Elves or Gnomes.
- **Does.** Three Dwarves, an armed fortress that shoots nearby monsters, and it unlocks Ballista Towers. Dwarves help with construction, are slow and very strong, and have the highest magic resistance. Good against Vampires and Dragons (fan).

### 4.3 Elven Bungalow
- **Requires.** Palace level 2, a Marketplace and an Inn. No Gnomes or Dwarves.
- **Does.** Two Elves per bungalow. It increases all Marketplace income (fan: it doubles it). It brings in an **Elven Lounge**, and a **Gambling Hall** if there is a Rogues Guild (one hall per Rogues Guild per the wiki, unsure).
- **Elf behaviour.** Elves are the best non-magical ranged fighters, fragile and expensive (fan). They are strongly drawn to money and have low willpower. They perform at Inns. They are easily bribed to explore or attack anything, even their own home (fan). They run from Guard Houses with Arrows (Carter, fan). The Elf wiki page lists them as disliking Dwarves and Gnomes, which is why you cannot mix.
- **Elven Lounge.** Pays no taxes. Heroes with low willpower waste both time and gold there, and the money is lost. High willpower heroes go less often. Monks "almost never" go. Elven Lounges may only be demolished by attack flag, and do not respawn (fan).
- **Gambling Hall.** Appears once there is a Rogues Guild and Elves. Pays no taxes (the in-game text says its ties to the Rogues Guild keep it off-limits to on-duty collectors). The fan wiki quotes the odds: blue wins 1 in 2 for double, red 1 in 5 for five times, gold 1 in 20 for twenty times, and a skull (all colours lose) 1 in 4 (fan). Heroes lose most of the time, though some leave richer. The player can bet treasury gold on the roulette wheel. Carter treats it as a last resort in A Deal with the Demon, and reloading saves is a cheat (fan).
- **Players say.** Elves are the best economic upgrade in time-limited gold quests (A Deal with the Demon, 100,000 gold in 40 days, can reach 5 to 6 thousand a day per Carter). Build the bungalow last, so the Lounge and Gambling Hall go up on the outside, and act as a buffer (fan). If you are under heavy attack, skip them (gameplay.tips, fan).

## 5. Self-generated structures

- **Houses.** Heroes' families build houses outside the Palace as you recruit. They take land you could use but make a little tax. Fan says each House adds 3 to each Marketplace's income. Carter says to take them off the repair and tax routes in quests with many houses (fan).
- **Fountain.** Appears as you build enough of the listed economic buildings. It gives an extra Tax Collector.
- **Gazebo.** A free Inn. Comes with Royal Gardens.
- **Graveyard.** Appears after enough of your heroes have died. It cannot be destroyed, and undead rise from it. The manual says bury fewer heroes by keeping them alive.
- **Sewer.** Appears as the kingdom grows. Spawns vermin, including Ratmen who ambush Tax Collectors. It cannot be destroyed.
- **Players say.** Use Guard Houses at Sewers and enclose the Marketplace (above).

## 6. Northern Expansion buildings

### 6.1 Outpost
- **Requires.** Palace level 2. In some quests you start with one.
- **Does.** A smaller, weaker command center. If the Palace falls you escape to an Outpost and keep ruling. It supports 2 Peasants, 1 Tax Collector, 2 Palace Guards and 6 heroes, and is a gold drop-off. It builds most guilds and shops. It unlocks Embassy, Magic Bazaar, Mausoleum and Hall of Champions without needing Palace levels, so the Outpost counts as a way to bypass the requirement. Carter notes an Outpost's six homes are a cheap way to add heroes in one-hero-per-guild quests (fan).

### 6.2 Embassy
- **Requires.** Palace level 2 or an Outpost.
- **Does.** Recruits veteran heroes at random intervals into empty guild slots, and charges gold each time. There is an on/off button. With an Outpost, any hero type is possible. Two homeless heroes can live there. In multiplayer it shares vision with allies.
- **Players say.** In "Legendary Heroes" (one hero per guild), Carter uses an Embassy and Outpost to add eight heroes for roughly 15,000 gold (fan).

### 6.3 Magic Bazaar
- **Requires.** Palace level 2 or an Outpost.
- **Does.** Six new magic items for heroes. Level 1: Tonic of Speed, Fire Balm. Level 2: Potion of Dirgo Strength, Elixir of Regeneration. Level 3: Invisibility Brew, Shapeshift Tincture. It also unlocks the Sorcerers Abode.
- **Players say.** Carter says Barbarians don't buy from it (fan). Fire Balm and similar items are for the northern monsters.

### 6.4 Sorcerers Abode
- **Requires.** A Magic Bazaar. One only. Self-built. Fan cost about 2,000 / 2,500 / 3,000.
- **Does.** Six sovereign spells with unlimited range. After each cast the sorcerer rests and a timer runs, so you can't chain casts from it.
  - Level 1: **Change of Heart** (a fleeing hero goes berserk and a fighting hero runs away) and **Frost Field**.
  - Level 2: **Dismiss** (teleports the target far away, or near another kingdom's Palace in multiplayer) and **Chain Lightning**.
  - Level 3: **Gate** (heroes near the abode are teleported one by one to the spot you pick) and **Earthquake** (damages every building in a large area).
- **Players say.** Carter uses Gate and Call to Arms to move paladin strike forces in Spires of Death. Gate leaves the sorcerer resting a long time (fan).

### 6.5 Mausoleum
- **Requires.** Palace level 2 or an Outpost.
- **Does.** A crypt for dead heroes. It holds a limited number, does not spawn undead, and revives long-dead heroes for gold that rises with the hero's level. If the hero's guild is full, or no Outpost or Embassy has room, the revived hero leaves your kingdom. If destroyed, the Mausoleum releases undead. Heroes of Agrela and Dauros don't use it.
- **Players say.** In the Balance of Twilight, resurrecting a level 50 sage from a mausoleum costs 50,000 gold (fan).

### 6.6 Hall of Champions
- **Requires.** Palace level 3 or an Outpost. One per kingdom.
- **Does.** You pick one monster type from those your heroes have killed. For a one-time fee all of that type are marked with a medallion and heroes treat them as more attractive targets. A timer shows how long the bounty lasts. Visitors get a temporary boost to maximum hit points and healing rate.
- **Players say.** It is a group flag instead of a per-monster flag, and doubles as an inn-like rest stop (Carter mentions "an inn or a hall of champions so they'll have a place to rest").

## 7. Reward flags

- **Two kinds.** An **Attack** flag goes on a visible monster, hero or building. It travels with a moving target. An **Explore** flag goes on any location, even the fog, and stays put.
- **Placing.** Set the amount on the Palace's Rewards panel and drop it on the map. Double-clicking a target uses your current amounts. Pressing Z on a selected enemy flags it.
- **Raising and removing.** Every click on plus raises the bounty by 100. It can never be lowered. You can remove a flag, but the gold in it is lost. You can't move a placed flag. The View Heroes button shows how many heroes are heading to it. It takes a little while for heroes to notice.
- **Paying out.** Gold goes to the hero or heroes who complete it. All heroes nearby share it.
- **Who responds.** Each hero weighs the gold against distance and danger. The manual says Rangers will not take on Skeletons for any price. Rogues and Elves are cheap to hire and are easy to buy for exploring (fan). Paladins and other pious heroes are expensive. Wizards, Rogues and Warriors take flags when you have no other work for them (Carter, fan). Barbarians usually ignore shop items but follow flags.
- **Dangers.** Heroes may flee from monsters they think they can't beat. Flagging a lair is how you destroy it.
- **Multiplayer.** Attack flags on an opponent's heroes or buildings declare war until all such flags are resolved. Heroes check loyalty before going for a flag on an ally's building.
- **Players say.** Flag the biggest threat when under heavy attack (manual's tip). Put explore flags at the edges to pull Rogues and Elves out. Put a 500-gold flag on each lair you find, per Carter (fan). In Fortress of Ixmil the fortress is ringed with poison plants, so heroes refuse the first flag. Wait for mid-level paladins (fan).

## 8. Sovereign spells

- **Casting.** You select a spell from the building or from the spell bar. The icon becomes your cursor, and it stays on the cursor until you right-click, so you can cast repeatedly. Each cast takes gold from the treasury. Temple and Sorcerer spells have unlimited range. Wizards Guild spells must be within range of a guild or Wizards Tower.
- **Targeting tip.** In a crowd, select the hero, pick the spell, then click the hero's portrait in the control bar. That prevents hitting an enemy by accident (manual and Carter).
- **Spells by source.** See each building above. In short:
  - Wizards Guild: Farseeing, Invisibility, Lightning Bolt, Anti-Magic Shield, Lightning Storm, Supercharge.
  - Agrela: Healing, Blessing, Resurrection.
  - Dauros: Stone Skin, Vigilance, Petrify.
  - Krypta: Wither, Animate Bones, Re-animate.
  - Fervus: Healing, Illusionary Hero, Vines.
  - Helia: Fire Strike, Sun Scorch.
  - Lunord: Winged Feet, Wind Storm.
  - Sorcerers Abode: Change of Heart, Frost Field, Dismiss, Chain Lightning, Gate, Earthquake.
- **How heroes react.** The spells act on heroes directly. Heroes don't choose them. Invisibility makes enemies lose the target, Anti-Magic Shield protects against Vampires and Evil Oculi, Healing and Resurrection save heroes, and Vigilance, Blessing, Stone Skin and Winged Feet boost them. Lightning Bolt and Earthquake can hit your own buildings (fan, Lightning Bolt can target buildings and friendly units).
- **Hero spells are separate.** Heroes cast their own spells whenever they like, with a recharge delay. Wizards learn Fire Blast, Meteor Storm and Magic Resistance at the Library. Healers heal. Priestesses drain life and raise skeletons. Cultists charm animals, camouflage and change shape. Adepts teleport short distances. Monks buff themselves. Paladins use Shield of Light.
- **Players say.** Lightning Storm stacks if cast repeatedly and hits only enemies (fan). Reanimate at 2,000 gold is cheaper than losing a high-level hero (fan). Gravestones last longer for higher levels.

## 9. Time pressure and pacing of a quest

- **Opening.** Most quests start with a Palace and a little gold. The manual says to build guilds and temples first, then recruit. You begin by exploring (Rangers), defending, and building a Marketplace so there is income.
- **Waves and clocks.** Typical clocks in Carter's guide (fan):
  - Day 3: first wave in The Day of Reckoning. Further waves on day 8 (elves), day 9 (dwarves), day 15 (vampires) and day 21 (Rock Golems and Dirgo).
  - Elven Treachery: 30 days to destroy all elven buildings or collect 50,000.
  - A Deal with the Demon: 100,000 gold in 40 days. Best time 27 days.
  - Trade Routes: 31 days.
  - Legendary Heroes: about 30 days to destroy all barrows.
  - Rise of the Ratmen: Rhoden appears around day 25.
- **Surges.** In Legendary Heroes, destroying a barrow causes an earthquake that damages every building and releases a wave of monsters. Other quests spawn monsters on a timer or on events (fan).
- **What a quest feels like.** The first few minutes are building and exploring. Then you put flags on lairs. Waves and bosses arrive on a schedule, and your economy has to be ready. The tools you use to cope are Call to Arms, resurrection, Market Day and Extortion.
- **After victory.** In the Northern Expansion you can keep playing, and special monster scripts start. You can also type cheat-like chat commands (NIGHT OF THE LIVING DEAD, PREPARE TO DIE, PUMP UP THE VOLUME, GOBLIN RUSH, GIVE ME ACTION) to spawn attacks.

## 10. Quest and scenario types

**Freestyle victory conditions (manual).**
- Eliminate All Foes: destroy all lairs.
- Gather Gold: first to the limit wins.
- Survive Specified Time: Palace still standing at the limit.
- Last Palace Standing: multiplayer, up to four kingdoms.
- You can also set starting gold, landscape, player and enemy forces, wandering monsters, a map number to repeat a layout, special events and a build-tree limit (Northern Expansion).

**Campaign objectives (Carter's list, fan).**
- Gold race: A Deal with the Demon (100,000 in 40 days). Elven Treachery (destroy all elves or 50,000 in 30 days).
- Destroy-all: Hold Off the Goblin Hordes (all goblin settlements). Urban Renewal (all Red guilds, lounges and gambling halls).
- Find and destroy one thing: Quest for the Crown (a tower), Slay the Mighty Dragon (sword site, then Vendral).
- Retrieve: Quest for the Magic Ring (the ring of healing). Brashnard's Ultimate Sphere of Power (seven shards).
- Survive: The Day of Reckoning, The Fertile Plain (seven waves).
- Boss kill: Vengeance of the Liche Queen, Rise of the Ratmen (Rhoden and the broken sewer mains).
- Escort or defend: Trade Routes (half the caravans). The Valley of the Serpents (destroy snake pits while keeping a bungalow alive). The Siege (Borjin's fortress or his caravans).
- Chain objectives: Balance of Twilight (resurrect the sage, restore the shrine, destroy the phantoms).
- Epic or unusual: Wrath of Krolm (altars or the avatar), Spires of Death (five spires), Legendary Heroes (barrows).

## 11. Small behaviours worth remembering

- Wizards learn spells at the Library and need a researched spell to learn.
- Rogues harvest the poisonous plants that Cultists plant (manual), and use the Rogues Guild for poison (level 2).
- Healers plant healing herbs that Rangers turn into healing potions (fan).
- Elves perform at Inns and Marketplaces for gold, and gamble and drink at their own Lounge. Monks and other high-willpower heroes hardly ever do.
- Elves and Rogues run from arrow-firing Guard Houses (fan).
- Healers follow Warriors and Tax Collectors, and may heal rival kingdoms' heroes.
- Barbarians rarely shop. Warriors of Discord almost never buy potions. Wizards and Paladins almost always do.
- Trolls hit marketplaces and caravans. Ratmen ambush Tax Collectors. Minotaurs raid markets (manual story).
- A dropped tax purse vanishes if no hero is near.
- Market Day closes the shop and pushes shoppers out. Fans use it to keep heroes out of the shop while enemies attack.
- Rangers vanish to distant lands when the map is fully explored, and come back if they survive.
- Gnomes leave if their homes are burned.
- Heroes divide a flag's reward among everyone nearby.
- The manual's story of the cloaked sovereign: a Ranger and a Warrior walk to the Blacksmith with their loot, and a Caravan rolls into the Marketplace.

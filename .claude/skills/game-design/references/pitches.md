# Seeds and pitches

## Generating seeds

Aim for about eight one-line seeds, deliberately from different starting points. Variety comes from where you start, not from tweaking one idea.

- **From an action he loves:** commanding at scale, building an engine from parts, steering one thing at speed, discovering a hidden rule, tending a system that makes its own stories, fishing in a calm place. Start from the action, then find the album whose world it belongs in.
- **From a form he loves:** a pinball table, a city builder, a roguelike, a fleet battle, a dig-and-surface loop. Ask what that form becomes when it is set inside one album, such as one table or one map per album.
- **From a world:** an album's setting (a retro-futurist city, an amphibian enchanted forest, a star survey, simulated history, mathematical objects). Ask what someone living there would do all day, and what would be fun about it.
- **One far pitch:** something unlike his list, chosen because an album's world asks for it. He chose "a mix" of near and far, so at least one pitch should surprise him.

Throw away these seeds:
- a famous game with his album art pasted on: build original designs, not recreations of existing games
- anything whose rules need the player to hear the music (`music.md` says why)

## Writing the pitch Henry sees

Two or three numbered lines, then one question. Nothing else: no headings, no feature lists, no "why this is great", no rule explanations. He asked to meet ideas as one-line pitches, with no reading beside them and no spin.

Each line is one sentence in the player's voice:
- what you are and what you do
- what makes it a game: the choice or tension at its heart
- the album whose world it's set in

End with a short question: which one, or none?

**Format example** (a generic illustration, not a recommendation):

> 1. You keep a lighthouse island running through a winter of storms, choosing each night which ships to guide in and which to leave to the rocks. (*Stellar Cartography*)
> 2. You run a frog village's market in the enchanted forest, trading dew and spells with travellers who each want something you can't quite spare. (*The Enchanted Forest of Naibihpma*)
> 3. You defend a city of tomorrow's power grid against a creeping blackout by rerouting current block by block. (*The Marvelous Electrical World of Tomorrow*)
>
> Which one should I build, if any?

Keep the selling language out. Write "you guide ships in", not "an epic voyage". If you think one pitch is weaker, drop it rather than ranking it.

Keep your concept pages and check results in a notes file, so you can answer "why?" if he asks, but don't send them unasked.

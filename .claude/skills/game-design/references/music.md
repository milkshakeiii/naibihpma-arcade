# Using Henry's music

The albums give each game its world, look and mood, and the tracks are its soundtrack. The music stays out of the mechanics.

## Why the mechanics leave the music alone

You can't hear the music. Any rule that depends on hearing it can't be checked by you: beats to hit, pitches to match, instruments as creatures, events timed to sections, key changes as signals. Henry has rarely seen such games work (he didn't like Crypt of the NecroDancer). He asked for all of it to be left out.

The game has to stand on its own as a game. A good test: it should play exactly the same muted, and the music should make it feel like a place.

## Where each album's world comes from

1. **Henry.** He wrote the albums. Before you lean on what an album is about, ask him for a line or two, and his words win.
2. **`music_listening_notes.md`** at the repo root. It covers five albums: *Abstract Objects*, *The Marvelous Electrical World of Tomorrow*, *The Enchanted Forest of Naibihpma*, *Stellar Cartography* and *Virtual History*.
   - Use its album-wide reads and each track's "thematic and emotional read" for setting, characters and mood. Ignore its harmony and timing detail, which is for the literal use you're avoiding.
   - An AI wrote it. It gets some track names wrong (it has "Doat", "Grof" and "Iron Age" where the files have "Daot", "Gorf" and "Stone Age"); the album zip's file names are correct.
3. **Track and album titles.** The zip in `~/Downloads` holds 43 WAV files named `... - NN Title.wav`. For the two albums the catalog lacks, *Background Music Radiation* and *Lost Wing*, the titles and Henry are what you have.

A one-line sense of each album from the catalog's reads (check with Henry before building on it):
- *Abstract Objects:* mathematical forms, drumless, cathedral-like.
- *The Marvelous Electrical World of Tomorrow:* optimistic retro-futurist city and railways.
- *The Enchanted Forest of Naibihpma:* an amphibian folklore forest, with Gorf the sorcerer and Twen the dew collector.
- *Stellar Cartography:* a survey of distant stars and rogue objects, moving inward to Vesta and Earth.
- *Virtual History:* civilization from prehistory to posthistory, as both lived history and a simulation.

## How the music is heard in a game

- **It's the soundtrack of a place.** Each table, level, world or region gets a track from the album it belongs to, and plays it while the player is there.
- **It plays through.** Failing doesn't cut or restart it. Henry's music shouldn't be chopped up by the player's mistakes.
- **No song-length clock.** A level doesn't end because its track ended. If the player is still playing when a track finishes, the next one starts.
- **Mood picks the activity, loosely.** A contemplative, drumless album suits calm, self-paced play. A driving one suits travel and speed. A frantic game over a quiet album reads as wrong at once. This is about the game's overall pace, not about reacting to the music moment to moment.
- **Sound effects stay quiet and few,** under the music, with timbres that suit the album's world. They aren't tuned or timed to the track.

## Lengths

Tracks run 3:08 to 7:17 (median about 4:50); the albums run about 24 to 44 minutes each. That matters for planning a sitting, such as one album as one evening, but never as a timer.

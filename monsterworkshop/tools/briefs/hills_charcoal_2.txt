This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is CHARCOAL WOOD, in the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. It is a ridge of pines where charcoal burners smoulder logs under earth mounds, and a stonemason works granite into hammers and anvils. Above it, behind the cliff along the top, are the hills' heights and the town of Verdigris, reached by THE STONE STAIR cut into the rock. On its right, the ridge ends in a cliff over the smelters of Bellows, off the right edge, and a living vine carries charcoal straight there over that edge.

This picture's layout already works: where things are, the road, the paths and the brook. Keep it close. Your part is to make it a real, smoky, working pine wood, with a grand stair and a vine that plainly does its job: dress it with decoration, give the cliffs character, and nudge things only a little.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Add decorations generously but tastefully, about the mounds, the mason's yard, the stair, the road and the brook. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground, like crops, or like the pines and junipers below, since those are things players pick up and harvest.
- There is no hedge on this screen. Keep open ground up to the left edge, the bottom edge, and the right edge below the cliff, with no hedge or wall along them: they run on into the screens around.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot from the road over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the signpost's name that's already there.

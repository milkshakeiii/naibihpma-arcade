This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is IRON BROOK, in the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. The Rust Brook comes down from the hemp fields above and turns west out of the hills here; iron lies in seams along its banks, which monsters dig with shovels. Pines grow on the far bank, and at the end of the road there's a sawpit, where logs are sawn into planks. Recompose the screen into a pleasant, green, piney riverside, a cheerful place to work, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground, like crops, or like the iron seams and pines below, since those are things players dig and fell.
- It is wild country: the top edge and the right edge are open, with no hedge, running on into the screens beside. Keep the dark hedge on the left and bottom edges, where the hills end. Keep the road and the brook where they cross the edges, on the rows and columns they cross on now. In between, the brook may wander a little and new paths may be laid.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the signpost's name that's already there.

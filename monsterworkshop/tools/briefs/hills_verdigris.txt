This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is VERDIGRIS, the town of the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods; it is named for the green that copper takes in the weather. It stands on the heights at the top of THE STONE STAIR, which climbs a cliff from the pine wood below; the high road runs through it, west to where a living vine lifts goods up the cliff, and east to the copper mine. It is the miners' and smiths' town. Recompose the screen into a cozy, sturdy, lived-in hill town of stone, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully, about the streets, the square, the yards and inside the buildings. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground or like crops, since those are things players pick up and harvest.
- It is a village: keep the dark hedge along the top and right edges. The left edge stays open, with no hedge, as it is now. The bottom edge is the top of the cliff above the pine wood: the hedge there may become a low stone parapet along the cliff's edge, but it must stay closed except where the Stone Stair comes up.
- Keep the road and the stair where they cross the edges. In between, streets may bend and new paths and cobbles may be laid.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot from the road over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the names already there.

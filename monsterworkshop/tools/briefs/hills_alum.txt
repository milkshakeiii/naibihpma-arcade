This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is ALUM SPRINGS, in the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. Under the cliff where the heights end, mineral springs well up on a stony terrace; as they dry they leave a crust on their rims, white alum at some and pale green vitriol at others, which monsters scrape off. Pyrite, fool's gold, shows in ledges at the cliff's foot. Beside the brine well stands the boiling house, where brine is boiled down to salt in wide pans, and pyrite with iron to green vitriol. A ramp climbs the cliff to the town above, and the middle road runs east along the Rust Brook. The springs are a workplace, not a spa or a garden: a mineral yard where the crust is scraped from the rims, raked, carried and boiled, as at an old alum or copperas works. Recompose the screen into a pleasant, practical working place, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground, like sacks, crystals or ore, or like the springs and pyrite ledges below, since those are things players gather.
- Nothing for leisure: no benches, stone lanterns, shrines, statues or seats by the springs. Everything added should look like it's there for the work.
- Colors stay calm and natural, as in the picture. Every spring's water is clear and blue, like the brook's; the alum and the vitriol show only as a crust on the springs' rims. The terrace is pale grey stone. No colored pools, no orange, yellow or green-stained ground, no glowing water.
- It is wild country: the left, right and bottom edges are open, with no hedge, running on into the screens beside. The cliff stays along the top edge, three tiles deep. Keep the ramp, the road and the brook where they cross the edges, on the rows and columns they cross on now. In between, new paths may be laid.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the building's name and the signposts' names that are already there.

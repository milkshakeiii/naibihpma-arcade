This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is BELLOWS, the smelters' town of the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. It lies in the valley of the Rust Brook, under cliffs: above it, the copper mine lowers ore down the top cliff on a living vine, and from the pine ridge on the left another vine carries charcoal across. Both set their goods down in the smelters' yard, where furnaces turn ore and charcoal into ingots. The town also has an assay office, where ingots are weighed and judged, and the miners' outfitter. Recompose the screen into a smoky, hard-working furnace town, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully, about the yard, the road, the brook and the buildings, inside them too. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground (no loose ore, ingots or charcoal lying about), since those are things players pick up.
- The top edge and the upper left are cliffs: natural rock, rough and weathered. Keep them. It is a town: keep the dark hedge along the right edge and the bottom edge. Keep the road, the brook, the trunk road south and both vines where they cross the edges, on the rows and columns they cross on now. In between, paths may bend and new ones may be laid.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot from the road over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the names already there.

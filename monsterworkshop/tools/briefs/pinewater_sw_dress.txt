This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. It is one quarter of PINEWATER, the weaving village where every player begins: cotton is picked in its fields, carded at a bench, spun into thread at a spinnery and woven into cloth at a loom house. The village has two such chains, one along its north road and one along its south street, so newcomers needn't crowd one. This is its south-west quarter, along the south street, where the second chain begins, and Old Spoke the wagonwright has his yard. The layout is settled: keep it exactly, and dress it. Make it a charming, cozy, lived-in part of a village, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would, with clusters, framing and some open grass, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Keep this layout. Every building, wall, door, field bush, bin, bench, mat, tool peg, signpost, road, path and lane stays on the tiles it is on now. Add decorations only on the open grass, each taking whole tiles, generously but tastefully.
- The dark hedge border runs along the left and bottom edges only. The top and right edges are open, because the village carries on into the screens there: draw no hedge, wall or fence along them.

- Leave walking room: everything must stay reachable on foot from the roads over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Decorations must not look like goods lying about (cotton bolls, thread, spools, cloth, logs, sacks) or like crops, since those are things players pick up and harvest.
- Draw no words or labels except the names already on the picture.

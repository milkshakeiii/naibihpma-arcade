This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is THE TIN GULCH, at the east end of the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. It is the only place in the world where tin is found. The high road from the town ends here, at the mouth of a narrow rocky gorge. Up the gorge the Rust Brook rises from under the rock, and black tin sand gathers on the gravel bars along its banks, where monsters dig it with a shovel and wash it in baskets in the brook. A prospector camps at the gorge's mouth. Recompose the screen into a wild, lonely, beautiful working place, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground (sacks, baskets, tools, ore, sand heaps, logs) or like crops, or like the tin sand bars, the iron seams and the springs below, since those are things players pick up and gather.
- Colors stay calm and natural, as in the picture. The brook's water is clear and blue everywhere, at its source too; no rust-red, orange or stained water. The iron shows only in its two seams.
- It is wild country: the left edge is open, with no hedge, running on into the town next door. The top and right edges are the gorge's grey rock, where the world ends. The bottom edge is the heights' edge: two rows of cliff all along it, with the brook falling through it.
- Keep the road where it crosses the left edge and the brook where it crosses the bottom edge, on the rows and columns they cross on now. In between, the gorge's walls may bend, the brook may wind, and paths may be laid.
- Keep two clear patches of open grass or gravel at the gorge's mouth, near the road, each at least 5 tiles wide and 3 deep, with nothing on them at all: monsters will build their own workshops there.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot over open ground, one tile wide at least.
- Draw no words or labels on the picture except the signpost's name already there.

What is here, and what must stay true:
- THE HIGH ROAD: it comes in at the left edge, on the row it does now, and ends at the gorge's mouth.
- THE GORGE: a narrow cleft with grey rock walls, running back from its mouth to its head, where the brook rises from under the rock. Its floor is gravel.
- THE RUST BROOK: clear water, two tiles wide, from its source at the gorge's head down the gorge, then turning south and falling through the cliff at the bottom edge, on the columns it falls through now. Keep a bridge, or stepping stones, across it to the far bank.
- THE TIN SAND: the ten low heaps of black, glinting sand on gravel bars beside the water. Keep ten, each beside the brook, each with walkable ground next to it.
- THE IRON SEAMS: the two rock ledges with a rust-red band, beside the brook near where it rises. Each keeps walkable ground next to it.
- THE SPRINGS: the two small round pools in stone rims on their patch of pale crust, one crusted white (alum), one pale green (vitriol), near the brook's source. Each keeps walkable ground next to it.
- THE PROSPECTOR'S CAMP: the canvas tent and the campfire with its kettle, by the road at the gorge's mouth. The small red thing by the tent is a basket lying on the ground: keep it where it is, on open ground.
- THE SIGNPOST: VERDIGRIS EAST by the road at the left edge.

Decorations that would suit it: a wooden sluice or rocker box on the bank where sand is washed; a drying rack and a stool by the tent; a woodpile for the fire; a cairn marking the claim; scree and fallen boulders at the walls' feet; ferns, moss and stones along the brook; rocks streaked rust where water seeps from the walls; junipers and twisted pines at the mouth; a few flowers in the grass.

This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is the north-east quarter of PINEWATER, the weaving village where every player begins: cotton is picked, carded at a bench, spun into thread and woven into cloth. Here the spinnery stands on the road, the gate bench beside it, where thread is twisted into rope, and the road leaves east through the hedge towards Tanglemere. In the top-right corner is the village's second cotton field, with its own bin and carding bench, so newcomers needn't all crowd the first. Today it is mostly bare grass. Recompose the screen into a charming, cozy, lived-in part of a village, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

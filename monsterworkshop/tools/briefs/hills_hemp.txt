This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is HEMP HOLLOW, in the ORE HILLS, a province of rocky hills, copper mines and dark pine woods. It is a green hollow on the Rust Brook, below the hills' heights, where the miners' side industry grows hemp, soaks the stalks in a retting pond and weaves coarse hemp cloth and sacks. Recompose the screen into a lived-in, working hollow, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully, about the field, the pond, the brook, the road and the hemp house, inside it too. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground or like crops, since those are things players pick up and harvest.
- Keep the dark hedge on the left edge, where the world ends. The other three edges are open: this screen runs on into the screens around it, so keep open ground up to those edges, with no hedge or wall along them. Keep the road and the brook where they cross the edges. In between, the road and the brook may bend, and new dirt paths may be laid.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot from the road over open ground, one tile wide at least.

What is here, and what must stay true:
- THE HEMP HOUSE: the stone building with its retting vat (the barrel on a red base), tan mats on either side of the vat where stalks, fiber and thread are laid, a mat with a lighter border below it where the work comes out, and two notices on the back wall where tools hang. A knife and a needle lie on its floor; they are goods, so leave them where they are. Keep its door, with a path to the road. It may move nearer the retting pond, since the stalks are soaked there.
- THE HEMP FIELD: the twelve tall green plants. Keep twelve, with open ground on every side of each, since monsters stand on them to cut stalks and set the stalks out beside them.
- THE RUST BROOK: it comes in at the right edge, runs left, and turns down out of the bottom edge. Keep where it crosses the edges.
- THE RETTING POND: the still water off the brook below the road, where hemp stalks soak. Keep it beside the brook.
- THE ROAD: it comes in at the top edge from the way into the hills, runs down and out of the bottom edge, and a road branches off it to the right along above the brook, out of the right edge to Charcoal Wood. Keep where both cross the edges.
- THE SIGNPOSTS: one by the road near the top (TEMPLE TOWN, up the road), one where the road branches (VERDIGRIS, the long way round, to the right).

Decorations that would suit it: hemp stalks bundled upright to dry, or laid on drying racks; bundles soaking in the pond, weighted with stones; a wooden scraping bench by the pond; a rail fence round the field with a gate; a scarecrow; a small shed; baskets and empty sacks; reeds and alders along the brook; boulders, dark pines and juniper bushes on the slopes; a bench by the water.

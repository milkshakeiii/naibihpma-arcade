This is one screen of a top-down pixel-art village game: 25 tiles across and 16 down, each tile 16 pixels, shown four times larger. The thin dark lines are the tile grid. This screen is one quarter of TANGLEMERE, the dyers' town by a lake, where cloth is dyed madder red and indigo blue. This is its quiet south-west corner, by the lake's shore and the woods. Recompose the screen into a charming, cozy, lived-in corner of the town, the way a fine level designer and pixel artist would: think about composition, framing, clusters and open space, so it reads as a real place rather than a diagram.

Rules for the picture:
- Keep exactly this art style, palette and pixel scale, top-down view, and keep the grid lines drawn. Everything sits on the grid: every object keeps its size in tiles.
- Use the pieces already in the picture, and add decorations generously but tastefully, scattered about the open ground and the shore as well as by and inside the building. Each decoration takes whole tiles. Decorations must not look like goods lying on the ground or like crops, since those are things players pick up and harvest.
- Keep the dark hedge border where it is, and keep the path where it crosses the top edge, since it joins this screen to the town above. Keep the lake where it crosses the right edge, since it goes on into the next screen. In between, the path may bend, new dirt paths may be laid, and the lake's shore may change shape.
- Leave walking room: everything below must be reachable on foot from the path over open ground, one tile wide at least.

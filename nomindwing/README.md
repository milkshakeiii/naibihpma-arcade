# No Mind Wing

A DOS-style arcade RTS: arcade on top, RTS underneath. You fly your king (mothership) yourself. It is the root of a tree of factories, and you spread that tree across the map with seeds. Everything is made of pixels: rocks, ships and the money that prints them.

**The fun thesis (Henry's words):** the mothership is the king and the root node of a tree of factories. You spawn off little self-propagating wings strategically, like a resource-collection dispatch or a forward production base. It's "the creativity of designing a TCG deck combined with the strategic thrill of outmaneuvering someone in chess."

There are two toys so far:

- **Proving ground** (the default): draw ships, set them loose, and see whether they fly and fight the way their drawings suggest. Iterated in rounds, logged in [ROUNDS.md](ROUNDS.md). From its yard, **MATCH** plays the match (below), planned in [MATCH.md](MATCH.md).
- **Toy 1** (`--toy1`): the first playable match, testing the tree of factories.

```
cargo run --release
cargo run --release -- --windowed
cargo run --release -- --toy1
```

# Proving ground

Toy 1's playtest found good feel and no depth. The planned fix is that strategy flows through the army you design, so every ship has to be drawn, not picked. The hard part, and where the 2020 version stalled ("sketchy enemy mind stuff"), is ship AI that works for any drawing. This toy tests one answer: a three-layer brain where none of the layers knows what kind of ship it is.

The proving ground is iterated in rounds. **[ROUNDS.md](ROUNDS.md)** has the goals, every round's additions, the verdicts and the parking lot. This is round 5.

1. **The order says where to go.** Each design has one: Hunt, Sab, Guard, Escort, Harvest or Hold. Sab (sabotage) goes for the other side's miners and factories first, past whatever guards them, and hunts when there are none. A ship keeps the enemy it's after until that one is gone or another is 30 px nearer, so two targets about as far off as each other don't have it turning from one to the other. Guard and Escort are match orders (wait at a post in the rings round what built it; form up on your king) and the order a design is drawn with is the one it starts a match on. The arena has no factory to guard and no king to escort, so a design drawn on either hunts there.
2. **The parts fire on reflex.** Flak turrets fire at the nearest enemy in range whatever the order, so an armed miner shoots back while it mines. A hunter with no guns left stays beside the nearest friend that still has some; guards and miners with no guns run home when hit.
3. **Its style says how it flies at what it fights; mass and thrust decide how well.** Integer inertia: velocity changes by whole quarter-pixels per tick, as fast as thrust over weight allows. There are three styles, and you pick one for a design in the yard:
   - **RUNS:** dive through the target, overshoot, loop back. A lance fires only straight ahead, like a rook, so a ship with one slides onto the target's row or column first and then dives along it ("LINING UP" on hover). It lines up on where the target will be when its shot gets there. A target that's lining a gun up on it in turn isn't led at all: the two settle on one line.
   - **KITE:** hold at the range of its guns (three quarters of a lance's or a beam's, or two thirds of a cannon's or a flak gun's) and back away from anything else that comes nearer than that. A lance ship lines up on the target's row or column the same way. It works on what the ship can outrun or outreach.
   - **ORBIT:** circle the target at that same distance, flat out, turning back the other way where the circle leaves the map or something solid is in the way. Fast and close, a ship gets round quicker than a cannon turns (42 degrees a second). A lance rarely bears.
   Until you pick, the drawing does: at least 1 thrust per weight runs, anything heavier kites. Without engines a ship stays put.

**Ship rules (since round 3):** a ship needs at least one core, and all its pixels must join up into one piece, edge to edge. Corners don't count. Every engine needs a straight 3-wide way out of the ship for its exhaust, like a rook's move: in one of the four directions, the engine's own line and the lines either side of it must be empty right out past the edge of the ship. So there's always a straight line of fire to every engine; plate can't fold round one (round 4; in round 3 the way out could bend). Loose pixels and buried engines show red in the yard, and the ship can't fly until they're fixed. Extra cores cost more each: the second +8, the third +16, the fourth +32. A ship's cost is its pixels plus its extra cores. In a fight, a ship dies when its last core goes. If it's cut apart, every piece that still holds a core flies on as a ship of its own, and pieces without one break away.

**Power (round 5):** hull makes it and engines and guns use it, from one pool per ship (each piece of a split ship runs on its own). A ship short of power runs everything at the fraction it has: at 75%, guns reload a third slower and engines push a quarter less. As hull is shot off, it weakens gradually. Short is allowed, just weaker; the yard shows "UNDERPOWERED", and the arena's hover shows the percentage.

**The beam, the shield, the missile, the repeater and the projector (2026-11-13)** are in the yard like any part. Five example ships show them at work (BEAMER, WARDEN, ARCHER for the missile, RAKER for the repeater, AEGIS for the projector), with nineteen ready-made fights. A sixth, the small NEEDLE, is in the library:

```
cargo run --release -- --parts
```

Nothing is loaded or saved there, so your own designs aren't touched and the examples can be redrawn freely. What to watch for:
- **A ship that can get off a warming beam's line does** ("DODGING A BEAM" on hover): flat out, square to the line, and it stays off until the beam has cut. A ship too wide or too slow to be clear in time doesn't try. The line moves with the beam's ship, so it's a race.
- **A ship with a beam flies like a lance ship**: it holds off at three quarters of its beam's reach and lines the gun up on the target's row or column.
- **A shield's circle is drawn as one outline** round all of a ship's circles, and thins by a quarter for each shot it has stopped.
- The beam takes a factory apart from beyond its flak's reach, and loses to anything that closes with it. A circle stops a lance, a shell, a missile or a beam as easily as a flak shot, so it's many small shots that wear it down: flak beats a shield, and a repeater does it from further off.
- **A missile needs no lining up,** so its ship flies however its card says: the ARCHER's says KITE, and it holds off at two thirds of a missile's reach. Against two ARCHERs an outpost's four flak guns shoot down a little over half the missiles and the outpost falls. A frigate's six shoot down nearly all of them.
- **A repeater ship flies like a lance ship:** it lines its gun up and holds off at three quarters of its reach. A circle that stops four shots and needs 3 s without one never gets its rest under a stream. The RAKER's gun is ten pixels: 140 px, a shot every 4 ticks. Plate turns every shot of it.
- **A projector's reach is a dotted line** round its lump. Every ship it covers wears a blue outline, which thins by a quarter for each quarter of the pool that's spent and goes when the pool is empty. A lump has a pale middle. Hovering a ship with one shows what it has left (PROJECTOR 32/38).
- **The AEGIS has no guns,** so it stays beside the nearest friend that has, as any unarmed ship on HUNT does, and it's drawn to be as fast as the other big ships. Small fast ships fly out of its reach in a couple of seconds: it's for a line of big ships, a king, or what's planted.
- **A projector does nothing for its own ship,** which is how you get at it: the ship's hull is bare unless another projector reaches it or it carries SHIELD pixels of its own, and no projector pixel is ever covered. Two projector ships side by side cover each other's hulls. The AEGIS's knob has a base five pixels wide so that one shell's 3x3 hole can't cut the whole knob off the ship. A knob on a thin neck goes to one hit.
- **What empties a projector is a stream.** Flak empties the AEGIS in about a second (it's back 3 s later). Missiles and other slow big shots at what it covers never do: a pool that rests between volleys is full for the next. They have to land on the projector's own ship.
- **With six weapons, the yard's part buttons run onto a second row.**

**Teams of nine, and the library (2026-11-12).** A team is nine designs (it was seven): nine tabs in the yard, nine rows in the arena, nine cards in a match. To fit, a row or a card is one line: icon, name and price. (A match's cards were on keys 1 to 9 and carried their order's letter until 2026-11-13. Now they're on Z X C V B N M , . and each shows its key at its left end.) A slot with nothing drawn says EMPTY. A save from when a team was seven fills the first seven slots.

**LIBRARY** in the yard (or L) opens the library over it. A drawing there carries its order, how it flies and its group size with it.
- **SHIPS:** every drawing you've kept, newest first, then the ready-made ones: the new parts' examples, the starter deck and the reference fleet.
- **TAKE** (Enter) puts a copy in the lit slot and goes back to the yard, where it's redrawn like any other. The library's copy stays as it is. What was in the slot is kept in the library first, unless one just like it is there already. **KEEP** (K) keeps the lit slot's drawing.
- **TEAMS** (Tab): whole teams of nine. LOAD swaps all nine slots, keeping the team you had first. KEEP TEAM keeps yours as TEAM 1, 2 and so on; RENAME (N) types it a name.
- **COPY** (C) and **PASTE** (V): a drawing is one line of text and a team is ten. COPY puts it on the clipboard. PASTE keeps whatever on the clipboard reads as drawings or teams and ignores the rest, so a line pasted out of a chat with words round it works. This is how two players pass designs today, with no service to run.
- **DELETE** (X) removes one of yours, on the second press. The ready-made ones stay.
- W/S or the arrows step through the list and the wheel scrolls it. 1 to 9, or the nine boxes, pick the slot. Esc, L or YARD closes it.
- Yours are saved in `design_library.txt` beside your team. The ready-made ones are never written there: they follow the game's rules as those change.

**Shipyard (round 4):** designs go up to 99x99, odd so the mirror has a centre column. The canvas zooms in whole steps with Q/E, the mouse wheel or the -/+ buttons: 1x is the ship at its real size, as in the arena; 2x shows the whole grid; 4x and 8x are for fine work, and at 8x each cell shows its part's shape. WASD or the arrows scroll. A design opens framed at the biggest zoom that shows all of it. Enter, or the ARENA and YARD buttons, swaps the yard and the arena (Tab did until 2026-11-10, when it became the next-part key in the yard; it still leaves the arena).

The right-hand column is icons and numbers, with the details on hover: cost, how it flies (RUNS, KITE or ORBIT: a button each, or Y) and how many of it leave together in a match (GROUP 1, 3, 6 or 12, or G), gauges for top speed, turning and thrust per weight (the gold mark is 1 thrust per weight, where attack runs start), a tally of the parts the ship has, and the status line.

Stats change as pixels are shot off: lose your engines and you slow down; lose hull and you may run short of power. Enemies bigger than you are solid, so you can't fly into them and shoot them from inside their plate. Ships move a pixel at a time, so they stop flush against whatever is solid to them and can't skip across something thin. An attack run peels off sideways before it would hit one, so small ships make strafing passes along a big ship's hull.

**Arena:** 480x360, seen through a 304x300 window. Scroll it with WASD, the arrows or the 1/6-scale minimap. Nine ready-made tests use the first seven of the nine design slots and are named from what's in them, so editing or renaming a design changes the tests. A tenth, A MIXED FLEET VS ITS TWIN, is made from your team as it stands: everything in it that flies and fights, on both sides. They're made for the starting library (the starter deck): the counter triangle, miners and a capital under attack, and three raids on a factory. A seeded design placed in the arena stands planted, as it would on its rock, so it's solid to every enemy ship. Keys 1-7 pick the design to place. The arena is deterministic: R replays exactly the same fight. A fight ends when one side is wiped out, after 3 minutes, or as a stalemate (a draw) once 20 seconds pass with nobody firing, nothing shot off and no armed ship closing in. The result names any side with no working guns left. Hover a ship to see its order, what it's doing, its target and where it's heading. Faint trails show recent flight paths; gold barrels show where cannons point; white sparks are shots bouncing off plate.

**Brains (an experiment, 2026-11-13).** Each side in the arena has a brain, on a button at the field's top left (B for blue's, V for red's). Changing one starts the fight again. With the same fleet on both sides (the twin fight), the brain is the only difference.
- **CARD:** as always. Each ship goes for the nearest enemy and flies at it the way its card says.
- **DRILLED:** before the fight, every design fights every enemy design at equal cost, each way it can fly against each way the other can, for up to a minute on two rolls of the dice. In the fight a ship flies at each enemy the way that did best over all that enemy's ways. The window says RUNNING DRILLS the first time, and remembers them for those drawings. Hover a ship to see what it learned about the one it's after.
- **ARCH:** one planner for the side. Before the first move it plays the fight forward 8 seconds for each way each of its designs could fly, on fresh dice, and keeps what came out best. Every 2 seconds after that it does the same for one design, in turn. Hover a ship to see what it decided for that design.

Nothing in either reads a part: both only watch fights. Neither is in the match yet.

**Headless tools:**
- `--brains [starter|parts|old|mine]`: the brains against each other, on mixed fleets from each deck (all four with no name given): what the designs learned in their drills, then five fights with a brain on one side and CARD on the other, sides swapped, 12 seeds each. It tries more brains than the window offers (drilled targets, and the cautious way of flying), and times the arch-AI's thinking.
- `--pg-sim parts`: the part examples' nineteen fights (see `--parts`), 12 seeds each, with the shots stopped at shields and the missiles shot down.
- `--pg-sim mine`: the arena's fights with your own saved team, read from `proving_ground_designs.txt`, so with the orders and styles your cards carry. It prints the team first and writes nothing. This is the one to run when the window shows something the other headless fights don't: they use the built-in decks.
- `--pg-sim`: the arena's reference fights, fought with the starter deck: each of its nine ready-made tests, then every pair of its four warships at the cost of the dearest (687 px a side), each on 12 seeds (seed 1 is the fight the window replays). Draws are split out into stalemates, and splits count the ships that broke in two. `--pg-sim old` fights the proving ground's own fleet (below) instead, in its own tests, as the five rounds were judged.
- `--pg-trace BLUE_SLOT N RED_SLOT N EVERY UNTIL`: ship-by-ship state. Use blue slot 99 to run test number RED_SLOT.
- `--match-sim N [--blue FILE] [--red FILE] [--bots BLUE,RED]`: N matches with nobody watching (seeds 1 to N), with how each ended, what each side made of each card and when it first had one, how many ships it had at 2:00 and 4:00, and a line every two minutes of the first. Each side plays the starter deck, or the designs in a save file such as `proving_ground_designs.txt`. `--bots` says who plays: `ai` (the default) or one of the scripted openings the AI is measured against, `clock` (round 1's opponent), `planter`, `expander`, `summoner`, `boomer` (a factory whenever the bank allows, and a king that prints miners without a pause), `rusher` (one factory, and a king that flies over to yours and prints lancers there until it has lost a quarter of itself) and `saboteur` (the AI's economy with every warship on SAB).
- `--judge N [--cards FILE] [--only NAMES]`: the AI against each scripted opening and against itself, N seeds on each side of the map. One line each: won, lost, drew, how long the matches ran, how many ships each side made, and when the AI's king was first slowed. `--cards` gives both sides the designs in a save file in place of the starter deck. `--only rusher,boomer` plays just those openings. `--whatif` judges the AI with the dispatcher on (below).
- **The dispatcher (an experiment, 2026-11-13).** `--judge N --whatif`, or `whatif` as a name in `--match-sim`'s `--bots`, gives the AI a dispatcher for its warships in place of its rules of thumb for waves. Every 20 seconds it plays the match itself forward a minute for each thing it could do (everyone home, everyone to one of the enemy's two nearest places, everyone to its own hardest-pressed place) and does what left it best off in pixels, keeping to a plan for a minute once it has changed to it. It's slow (about half a second a look late in a match) and headless only: the window's AI doesn't use it. Set `NMW_TRACE` to see each thought on stderr.
- `--pace N`: how fast a match runs away, on seeds 1 to N: the boom against the AI, and the AI against itself. For each, who won and how long the matches ran, and for each side the factories it had planted and the pixels it had mined by 3:00, 5:00 and 7:00, when its second factory went down, and the ships it made (medians).
- `--deck [FILE]`: what each card of a deck is (size, cost, power, speed, guns, build order and how long one round of it takes), for the starter deck or a save file.
- `--export-screenshots DIR`: proving-ground and match screenshots.

## The match (Match round 2)

A 1v1 against the AI on a mirrored 1400x1050 map: kill its king. From the yard, **MATCH** asks whether you play your team of nine as your deck or the starter deck; the AI plays the starter deck. Your first seven slots start out as the starter deck, so there's a working deck to redraw from the first minute. Each side starts with its king and a 190 px bank, nothing else. [MATCH.md](MATCH.md) has the plan, the numbers and what each round is testing.

A right-click on the factory itself takes the rally point away. The ships it built before keep the home they had, and miners mine wherever their home is. With ships and factories both selected, the ships are sent and the factories get the rally point. A king has no rally point: a right-click sends it. |
| G / LET GO | The selected ships are unsent again: they do what the stance says, so on GUARD they go home. The button is by the clock, after the stance, while anything is selected. |
| Ctrl+1 to 9, 1 to 9, Shift+1 to 9 | Control groups. Ctrl and a number makes that group of what's selected. The number alone selects the group, and pressed twice running takes the view to it. Shift and a number adds what's selected to the group. |
| The small map | Click or drag on it to move the view. The view doesn't follow the king any more. |
| K, or the note above the small map | Selects your king. Again straight away, the view goes to it, as with a group's number. |
| Tab / MAP | The whole map in the field at a fifth of its size. You can plant, select and send from it. A click on it selects; it doesn't move the view. |
| Space / PAUSE, F / speed, F1 | Pause, 1x/2x/4x, the rules. |
| F3 | The keys page: every key and mouse button the screen in front of you takes. The yard, the arena and the library have one each too, and the rules panels have a button for it. Any key or a click puts it away, and a match against the AI stands still meanwhile. Each page is one table in `src/pg/keys.rs`, and a test reads each screen's key handler and fails if a key it takes isn't in that screen's table. |
| L / LAG | The lag dial, at the top of the field: how many ticks your commands wait before they're applied (see *Against a person*). |

- **A beaten king is slow.** After the first minute, a king whose side has under 40 px of ships and factories standing (everything of its team but the king itself) flies at a fifth of its speed: 7.5 px/s, not 37.5. That is slower than the slowest ship a drawing can make, so anything with an engine catches it. It's back to full speed the moment its side is back to 40 px: a seed in flight and a factory still growing count, the bank doesn't. The field says so for either king, a slowed king's engines go dark, and the two lines under the small map show SLOW; hover a king for how much its side has standing. At full speed a king is as fast as any ship a drawing can make, and faster on a diagonal (the starter deck's fighters make 37 px/s), so one with nothing left to lose could otherwise run until the 40-minute draw.
- **Standing orders.** HUNT goes after the nearest enemy, wherever it is. SAB goes after the nearest enemy miner or factory (anything with a scoop or a factory pixel on it, or planted; never a king), past whatever guards it, and hunts when there are none left. GUARD waits at a post round what built the ship (below) and fights what comes within 160 px of its builder's place. ESCORT forms up in rings round your king, flies where it flies and fights what comes within 110 px of it; a king at full speed outruns its escort, which catches up when the king slows. A design starts a match on the order it was drawn with. The starter deck's warships are drawn on GUARD, so they gather round their factory until you send them.
- **Groups.** A design given a group size of 3, 6 or 12 in the yard doesn't leave on HUNT or SAB one at a time. Its ships wait at their posts, guarding ("WAITING FOR ITS GROUP" on hover), until that many of them are waiting within 96 px of one another; then those go, and one built later waits for a group of its own. Sent out from GUARD or ESCORT, they gather first, and a group already gathered leaves at once. Switched between HUNT and SAB, the ones already out stay out. A design's style and group size are fixed for the match: hover its card to read them. In a match the only thing you switch is the order.
- **Posts.** A ship with nothing to do waits at a post of its own: a place in the rings round what built it, which is its factory, or the spot where your king summoned it. Guards fill the rings in the order they were built. The first ring is 16 px outside the builder, clear of what it prints next, and rings and posts are about 16 px apart, so an outpost's first eight guards stand all round it and the ninth starts a second ring. At its post a ship looks outward and sits still. It gives way only to what can't move aside for it (a factory, a ship being printed, a king) and to a ship already waiting that was built before it: then it waits at the nearest place clear of them all (of two as near, the one further from home), and goes back when the way is free. Whatever flies past goes round it, except a miner at work, which flies over. A guard that leaves to fight comes back to the post it had; the rings close up when one is lost. Ships your king summons within 24 px of posts already kept join those rings.
- **Designs are built or seeded** (yard: BUILT / SEEDED). A seeded design's core stays at the grid's centre, where its seed lands, and it grows outward from there. It needs a rock at least as big as itself, or it stays unfinished. A seeded design may have engines and guns: it grows out of its rock, then flies off when it's whole.
- **Factory pixels** (Industry, 2 power each) print a design's build order (yard: BUILDS / ADD, by deck slot). Each prints 1 pixel about every 27 s, so the outpost's 28 print 1.05 px/s, and each lets the factory build ships up to 10 px bigger. It builds the next thing on its list that it's big enough for, pays as it prints, and waits when the bank is empty; printers sharing a bank take turns, a pixel each. Nothing grows, prints or arrives by hyperspace into an enemy's pixels: it waits or grows round. Carriers (built ships with factory pixels) print the same way.
- **Pixels come from rocks.** A ship with two or more scoops bites a pixel out of a rock every 3.3 s and hauls a full load to the nearest node. A miner at work flies straight to its rock pixel and straight home with a full load: it keeps apart from nobody and nobody keeps apart from it, so miners heap up on a rock face and fly through each other. Each bite sparks on the rock, and a scoop turns the colour of loose pixels for each scoopful aboard, so you can see a miner working and how full it is. Your king also makes one pixel every 4 s by itself, so a side that has lost every miner can still print one.
- **The network:** your king and anything with factory pixels are nodes. Nodes store pixels and link (dotted lines) when within reach of each other, and linked nodes share a bank. Miners haul to the nearest node. Cut a link and the bank splits by where the pixels sit; a destroyed node spills what it held. A note says so when your network breaks in two: two nodes parting, or your king cut off by a node you lost. Your king flying out of reach by itself breaks nothing, and says nothing. BANK is what your king's network holds; a `+N` beside it is what nodes cut off from your king hold. Fly near them, or plant a link, to spend it.
- **Rocks have no owners.** Anyone can mine any rock, and a seed can eat any rock. The planted factory is the claim.
- **Factories stay put, keep apart and are solid.** No factory grows into another, yours or the enemy's: a seed sent where one already stands comes down beside it and grows what it has room for (the planting bar warns you first). Whatever is planted on a rock is solid to every enemy ship, whatever its size: a king flown at an enemy factory stops against its plate. Your own ships fly over your own factories, and your miners work the rock under them and under any ships parked there. A bigger ship does pass over a drifting hulk with no engines, without pushing it. Rocks themselves stop nothing.
- **The AI** plays by the same commands you do. It plants a factory when its bank holds the price (and never lets you have more factories than it has for long), keeps one more miner than it has factories, masses its warships on GUARD, and sends them out as a wave (the slow designs first, so they arrive together) when it's the stronger side or has rested long enough. A raider within 200 px of its king, anything it has planted or one of its miners turns the guards out, and they stay out until every raider is 60 px further off than that. Your king counts as a raider from the moment it crosses to the AI's half of the map. Its king prints what counters your fleet: something big against small flak ships, a flak swarm against small lance ships, lances against big ships (and against your king, if it comes alone).
  - **When it arms.** A raid that outweighs every warship it has comes before miners. While your fleet is a quarter bigger than its own, guns come before what it's saving for a factory. Otherwise it prints miners first and guns with what's left over.
  - **Where its king stands.** While a raid is on, its king moves beside the factory nearest its start, so the factory's flak and its own cover each other (unless moving would cut one of its factories off from its bank). It stays there against anything it and the armed friends round it outweigh. Outweighed, it falls back on the nearest of its factories with no enemy gun near, and with none it backs straight away.
  - **Where it plants under a raid.** Against a raid it outweighs, as on any day. Against one that outweighs it, never on a rock the raiders are at, and only where the new factory joins its network.

For anyone changing the sim: every part is one row of `PART_INFO` (`src/pg/design.rs`); every rule about where pixels may be is in `src/pg/sim/space.rs`; all match input is a `Cmd`; the arena and the match draw the world through `src/pg/scene.rs`. `cargo test --release` runs soak tests that audit every tick of every arena test, an AI match and four matches driven by random commands (`src/pg/sim/audit.rs`).

Sessions append to `proving_ground.log` (each run's designs and result, and each match's start, a line a minute and its result). A crash writes a `CRASH` line there, with where in the code and what it said, and a session that ends properly writes `SESSION END`: a log that stops with neither was stopped from outside. Your library of kept drawings and teams is `design_library.txt`. Designs persist in `proving_ground_designs.txt`, each trimmed to its drawing with its place on the grid (round 2 and 3 files, which saved the whole 41x41 grid, load centred); round-1 designs (15x15, old rules) were moved aside to `proving_ground_designs_round1.txt`. A saved design still exactly like an old default is swapped for the current one when it loads. All of these files are gitignored.

### Against a person (the network spike)

Two games play one match, each player with their own team of nine, on one machine or two. It's a spike: enough to find out how a match feels with lag, and whether two machines stay in step. There's an address to type and nothing else: no list of games, no second go, no pause.

- **To play:** on the match menu one player picks **HOST A MATCH** and the other **JOIN A MATCH**, types the host's address (it starts as 127.0.0.1, which is this machine) and presses Enter. The host plays blue, the joiner red. From a command line it's `--host [PORT]` and `--join ADDR[:PORT]`; `--host local` hears only this machine, and `--windowed` puts a game in a window, so two fit on one screen. The port is 7777, TCP. To host for another machine that port has to be open to it, and Windows asks about its firewall the first time.
- **How it works:** each machine runs the whole match, and all they send each other is commands. A command is applied a few ticks after it's given (the lag), so the other machine has it in time, and a tick is played only when a machine holds both sides' commands for it. A machine still short of the other's waits, and says WAITING FOR THE OTHER PLAYER.
- **The lag dial** (L, or LAG at the top of the field): how many ticks your own commands wait. A tick is 67 ms. A network match starts at 2 and goes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8; with less than the connection needs, the match stutters. Against the AI the dial is there too, from 0: that's how a network match of that lag would feel, with no network.
- **The check:** once a second both machines hash the whole match and compare. IN STEP and a time, at the top of the field, is the last second they agreed on. If they ever differ it says OUT OF STEP and since when. From there the two screens show different games: that's a fault in the game, and the log has the tick.
- **What a network match can't do:** pause, run fast, or stop while the rules or the quit question are up. LEAVE ends it for both players. If the other player goes, it ends with no result.

**The starting library.** With no save file, your first seven slots are the starter deck, and slots 8 and 9 are empty: FIGHTER, LANCER, FRIGATE, MINER, OUTPOST, SHIPYARD, CAPITAL. They are Henry's drawings of 2026-11-10: every card spends all the power its hull makes, so none carries pixels that do nothing (`--deck` prints the sheet). The miner was redrawn once more that day, after eighteen drawings were played against each other. On 2026-11-11 a lance, a factory pixel and a scoop each came to use more power (and a scoop to weigh 2), and the five cards that left short were given the hull they need, shapes and sizes kept: the lancer is 9 px, the miner 9 px with two engines, the outpost has 28 factory pixels and the shipyard 118. Those five are stand-ins, drawn by rule and not by play. A save still holding an untouched card from before gets today's when it loads. Delete `proving_ground_designs.txt` to get it back. The fleet the rounds were judged on (fighter, lancer, frigate, destroyer, capital, gunminer, titan) lives on in the code with its own nine tests. Its drawings carry hull that feeds nothing, so since 2026-11-11 nothing is measured on it: `--pg-sim` fights the starter deck, and `--pg-sim old` gives the old fleet's numbers, which a change to the arena's rules would still show up against. The checks fly both. The designs drawn during the rounds were moved aside to `proving_ground_designs_before_match_deck.txt` on 2026-11-10. To have one back, copy its line over the line of the slot you want it in (the file is a header, then slots 1 to 7 in order).

## Rules in one breath

- Pixels are money. Every design costs its pixel count, paid one pixel per frame as it prints.
- Collectors chew pixels off rocks and wrecks and haul them to the nearest factory.
- A seed flies to a rock and eats it into a base, pixel by pixel.
- A base keeps its pinned build order alive, rebuilding whatever dies:
  - **Outpost:** 2 collectors, 1 escort, and 1 outpost seed. The seed means outposts spread.
  - **Forward base:** 2 escorts and 3 raiders.
- Raiders wait at their factory until there are 3, then the pack hunts.
- Every colour is a part: yellow shoots, blue-white pushes, orange scoops, grey takes two hits, and a ship dies when its white core is gone.
- Shots knock pixels loose for anyone to collect.

## Tools

- `--sim N`: plays N AI-vs-AI matches and N AI-vs-idle-king matches headless, then prints the stats.
- `--trace SEED [--both]`: prints one match's state every 20 seconds.
- `--export-screenshots DIR`: 2x PNGs of the main screens.

Playtests append to `nomindwing_playtest.log` (gitignored): every card played, every base planted or lost, and a snapshot every 30 seconds.

## Tuning notes (2026-11-07)

- **Map:** 960x720. Every rock has a mirror twin through the centre. An earlier centre rock sat 0.35 px closer to one king, and that king won 5 of 6 AI matches.
- **Aiming:** gunners aim at a random surviving pixel. Aiming at the centre drilled a straight tunnel through a king that later shots flew through without touching anything.
- **Pacing:** the AI builds economy first, plants its forward base at about 1:50, prints no raiders before 1:40, and doesn't send its king before 3:20. An idle king falls at about 2:10. AI-vs-AI matches end in 3½–6½ minutes, roughly 50/50.

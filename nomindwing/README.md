# No Mind Wing

A DOS-style arcade RTS: arcade on top, RTS underneath. You fly your king (mothership) yourself. It is the root of a tree of factories, and you spread that tree across the map with seeds. Everything is made of pixels: rocks, ships and the money that prints them.

**The fun thesis (Henry's words):** the mothership is the king and the root node of a tree of factories. You spawn off little self-propagating wings strategically, like a resource-collection dispatch or a forward production base. It's "the creativity of designing a TCG deck combined with the strategic thrill of outmaneuvering someone in chess."

There are two toys so far:

- **Proving ground** (the default): draw ships, set them loose, and see whether they fly and fight the way their drawings suggest. Iterated in rounds, logged in [ROUNDS.md](ROUNDS.md). From its yard, **MATCH** plays the match (below), planned in [MATCH.md](MATCH.md).
- **Toy 1** (`--toy1`): the first playable match, testing the tree of factories.

```
cargo run --release
cargo run --release -- --windowed
cargo run --release -- --toy1
```

# Proving ground

Toy 1's playtest found good feel and no depth. The planned fix is that strategy flows through the army you design, so every ship has to be drawn, not picked. The hard part, and where the 2020 version stalled ("sketchy enemy mind stuff"), is ship AI that works for any drawing. This toy tests one answer: a three-layer brain where none of the layers knows what kind of ship it is.

The proving ground is iterated in rounds. **[ROUNDS.md](ROUNDS.md)** has the goals, every round's additions, the verdicts and the parking lot. This is round 5.

1. **The order says where to go.** Each design has one: Hunt, Sab, Guard, Escort, Harvest or Hold. Sab (sabotage) goes for the other side's miners and factories first, past whatever guards them, and hunts when there are none. A ship keeps the enemy it's after until that one is gone or another is 30 px nearer, so two targets about as far off as each other don't have it turning from one to the other. Guard and Escort are match orders (wait at a post in the rings round what built it; form up on your king) and the order a design is drawn with is the one it starts a match on. The arena has no factory to guard and no king to escort, so a design drawn on either hunts there.
2. **The parts fire on reflex.** Flak turrets fire at the nearest enemy in range whatever the order, so an armed miner shoots back while it mines. A hunter with no guns left stays beside the nearest friend that still has some; guards and miners with no guns run home when hit.
3. **A ship that fights holds at the reach of its guns.** It holds at three quarters of a lance's or a beam's reach, or two thirds of a cannon's or a flak gun's, with the gun it lines up (a lance, a beam, a repeater) pointed at its target. Without engines a ship stays put. (Until 2026-11-16 a planner picked one of three set ways for each ship, attack runs, kiting or orbiting, by playing the fight forward. That and the three ways are gone: see *Nose-first flight* below, and the git history for what was there.)

**Ship rules (since round 3):** a ship needs at least one core, and all its pixels must join up into one piece, edge to edge. Corners don't count. Every engine needs a straight 3-wide way out of the ship for its exhaust, like a rook's move: the other way from the way it pushes (astern, for an engine as it comes; since 2026-11-16, when engines got a way to push, where any of the four directions used to do), the engine's own line and the lines either side of it must be empty right out past the edge of the ship. So there's always a straight line of fire to every engine; plate can't fold round one (round 4; in round 3 the way out could bend). Loose pixels and buried engines show red in the yard, and the ship can't fly until they're fixed. Extra cores cost more each: the second +8, the third +16, the fourth +32. A ship's cost is its pixels plus its extra cores. In a fight, a ship dies when its last core goes. If it's cut apart, every piece that still holds a core flies on as a ship of its own, and pieces without one break away.

**Power (round 5):** hull makes it and engines and guns use it, from one pool per ship (each piece of a split ship runs on its own). A ship short of power runs everything at the fraction it has: at 75%, guns reload a third slower and engines push a quarter less. As hull is shot off, it weakens gradually. Short is allowed, just weaker; the yard shows "UNDERPOWERED", and the arena's hover shows the percentage.

**Parts**, in the yard's seven categories (keys 1-7; key 7 is HEAVY WEAPONS, since 2026-11-17, when an eighth weapon's name no longer fitted WEAPONS' two rows of buttons; Tab, or the same key again, steps to the category's next part). Key 4 is UPGRADES, parts that make other parts better. It was POWER, which stayed empty (hull is the power) until an eighth weapon part didn't fit the weapons' two rows of buttons.

**Piercing and armor (2026-11-15):** one rule. A shot breaks a pixel only if it pierces more than the pixel's armor; otherwise it bounces off and stops there. Pixels have no hit points, so that's all a hit is. Armor: bare 0, plate 1, heavy plate 2. Piercing: flak, repeaters and trackers 1, lances and missiles 2, cannons and beams 3 (the most there is, so everything has an answer), and a gun touching a piercer one more, up to 3. A shot that goes on through (a lance, a beam) has a depth (a lance's is 6, a beam's 4 for each of its pixels): each pixel it breaks uses up 1 and the pixel's armor more (bare 1, plate 2, heavy plate 3), and it stops once that's spent (the pixel that spends it still breaks) or at the first pixel it can't break. A burst (a shell, a missile) goes off wherever it meets a ship and breaks every pixel in its 3x3 whose armor it out-pierces.

**The beam, the shield, the missile, the repeater and the projector (2026-11-13)** are in the yard like any part. Five example ships show them at work (BEAMER, WARDEN, ARCHER for the missile, RAKER for the repeater, AEGIS for the projector), with nineteen ready-made fights. A sixth, the small NEEDLE, is in the library:

```
cargo run --release -- --parts
```

Nothing is loaded or saved there, so your own designs aren't touched and the examples can be redrawn freely. What to watch for:
- **A ship that can get off a warming beam's line does** ("DODGING A BEAM" on hover): flat out, square to the line, and it stays off until the beam has cut. A ship too wide or too slow to be clear in time doesn't try. The line moves with the beam's ship, so it's a race.
- **A ship with a beam flies like a lance ship**: it holds off at three quarters of its beam's reach and lines the gun up on the target's row or column.
- **A shield's circle is drawn as one outline** round all of a ship's circles, and thins by a quarter for each shot it has stopped.
- The beam takes a factory apart from beyond its flak's reach, and loses to anything that closes with it. A circle stops a lance, a shell, a missile or a beam as easily as a flak shot, so it's many small shots that wear it down: flak beats a shield, and a repeater does it from further off.
- **A missile needs no lining up,** so its ship just holds off: the ARCHER, at two thirds of a missile's reach. Against two ARCHERs an outpost's four flak guns shoot down a little over half the missiles and the outpost falls. A frigate's six shoot down nearly all of them.
- **A repeater ship flies like a lance ship:** it lines its gun up and holds off at three quarters of its reach. A circle that stops four shots and needs 3 s without one never gets its rest under a stream. The RAKER's gun is ten pixels: 140 px, a shot every 4 ticks. Plate turns every shot of it.
- **A projector's reach is a dotted line** round its lump. Every ship it covers wears a blue outline, which thins by a quarter for each quarter of the pool that's spent and goes when the pool is empty. A lump has a pale middle. Hovering a ship with one shows what it has left (PROJECTOR 32/38).
- **The AEGIS has no guns,** so it stays beside the nearest friend that has, as any unarmed ship sent out to fight does, and it's drawn to be as fast as the other big ships. Small fast ships fly out of its reach in a couple of seconds: it's for a line of big ships, a king, or what's planted.
- **A projector does nothing for its own ship,** which is how you get at it: the ship's hull is bare unless another projector reaches it or it carries SHIELD pixels of its own, and no projector pixel is ever covered. Two projector ships side by side cover each other's hulls. The AEGIS's knob has a base five pixels wide so that one shell's 3x3 hole can't cut the whole knob off the ship. A knob on a thin neck goes to one hit.
- **What empties a projector is a stream.** Flak empties the AEGIS in about a second (it's back 3 s later). Missiles and other slow big shots at what it covers never do: a pool that rests between volleys is full for the next. They have to land on the projector's own ship.
- **With six weapons, the yard's part buttons run onto a second row.**

The other twenty-four show the five teams with a strategy, before they're played in a match; N goes to the next. Each is between two ready-made teams, and each side's slots are its own team's, all of it: the list beside the field is blue's team, or red's while your clicks place for red (T). Your own team is untouched, and going to the yard and back brings you to the first fight. (Until 2026-11-14 the arena's fights were made for the starter deck's slots. For each team in turn: its army against the starter deck's, then a fight or two showing what its new drawings are for (a nest under lancers, corvettes against lancers, a cruiser against frigates, cruisers at a dock, a battleship against lancers, wasps at miners and at fighters, a lancet against a battleship and against a swarm). **Teams of nine, and the library (2026-11-12).** A team is nine designs (it was seven): nine tabs in the yard, nine rows in the arena, nine cards in a match. To fit, a row or a card is one line: icon, name and price. (A match's cards were on keys 1 to 9 and carried their order's letter until 2026-11-13. Now they're on Z X C V B N M , . and each shows its key at its left end.) A slot with nothing drawn says EMPTY. A save from when a team was seven fills the first seven slots.

**LIBRARY** in the yard (or L) opens the library over it. A drawing there is its pixels, its name, whether it's seeded and what it builds: nothing else.
- **SHIPS:** every drawing you've kept, newest first, then the ready-made ones: the new parts' examples, the starter deck and the reference fleet.
- **TAKE** (Enter) puts a copy in the lit slot and goes back to the yard, where it's redrawn like any other. The library's copy stays as it is. What was in the slot is kept in the library first, unless one just like it is there already. **KEEP** (K) keeps the lit slot's drawing.
- **TEAMS** (Tab): whole teams of nine. LOAD swaps all nine slots, keeping the team you had first. KEEP TEAM keeps yours as TEAM 1, 2 and so on; RENAME (N) types it a name.
- **COPY** (C) and **PASTE** (V): a drawing is one line of text and a team is ten. COPY puts it on the clipboard. PASTE keeps whatever on the clipboard reads as drawings or teams and ignores the rest, so a line pasted out of a chat with words round it works. This is how two players pass designs today, with no service to run.
- **DELETE** (X) removes one of yours, on the second press. The ready-made ones stay.
- W/S or the arrows step through the list and the wheel scrolls it. 1 to 9, or the nine boxes, pick the slot. Esc, L or YARD closes it.
- Yours are saved in `design_library.txt` beside your team. The ready-made ones are never written there: they follow the game's rules as those change.

**Shipyard (round 4):** designs go up to 99x99, odd so the mirror has a centre column. The canvas zooms in whole steps with Q/E, the mouse wheel or the -/+ buttons: 1x is the ship at its real size, as in the arena; 2x shows the whole grid; 4x and 8x are for fine work, and at 8x each cell shows its part's shape. WASD or the arrows scroll. A design opens framed at the biggest zoom that shows all of it. Enter, or the ARENA and YARD buttons, swaps the yard and the arena (Tab did until 2026-11-10, when it became the next-part key in the yard; it still leaves the arena).

The right-hand column is icons and numbers, with the details on hover: cost, whether the ship flies or stays put, gauges for top speed, turning and thrust per weight (as a match flies: the gold mark is 1 thrust per weight), a tally of the parts the ship has, and the status line.

Stats change as pixels are shot off: lose your engines and you slow down; lose hull and you may run short of power. Enemies bigger than you are solid, so you can't fly into them and shoot them from inside their plate. Ships move a pixel at a time, so they stop flush against whatever is solid to them and can't skip across something thin.

**Arena:** 480x360, seen through a 304x300 window. Scroll it with WASD, the arrows or the 1/6-scale minimap. Nine ready-made tests use the first seven of the nine design slots and are named from what's in them, so editing or renaming a design changes the tests. A tenth, A MIXED FLEET VS ITS TWIN, is made from your team as it stands: everything in it that flies and fights, on both sides. They're made for the starting library (the starter deck): the counter triangle, miners and a capital under attack, and three raids on a factory. A seeded design placed in the arena stands planted, as it would on its rock, so it's solid to every enemy ship. Keys 1-7 pick the design to place. The arena is deterministic: R replays exactly the same fight. A fight ends when one side is wiped out, after 3 minutes, or as a stalemate (a draw) once 20 seconds pass with nobody firing, nothing shot off and no armed ship closing in. The result names any side with no working guns left. Hover a ship to see its order, what it's doing, its target and where it's heading. Faint trails show recent flight paths; gold barrels show where cannons point; white sparks are shots bouncing off plate.

**Nose-first flight (2026-11-16; `--nose` is a toy for watching it).** A ship's engines push it only the ways they're drawn to, it keeps the speed it has, and rotation engines turn it. Everything flies this way, in the arena and in a match, except a king, which still flies straight to where it's sent at a set speed.
- **A miner flies on to the rock it's eating and works what's in reach of its scoops from there** (8 px, or as near as it can place itself twice over: 12 for the starter miner), picks up loose pixels within that, and hands its load over from that far. It stops at its drop-off. A seed comes down on the rock pixel it was sent to once it has come to rest as near that as it can place itself (3 px). (That's the arena, where a station takes a load at once. In a match a tractor takes it, a pixel at a time: see "Pixels come from rocks" below.)
- **A ship flies straight at where it's going** (since 2026-11-16; it used to fly across and then along). It asks for speed both ways at once, stops the shorter way first and then the longer, each in one burn, and stops as near the place as a ship of its sort can place itself (3 px with rotation engines, 6 for the starter fighter, 14 for a frigate with none; usually nearer). Stopped that near, it stays: it can't shuffle.
- **A lance fires at where its target is heading:** when its shot would meet a standing pixel of an enemy that flies on at the speed it has.
- **A gun aims at a pixel it can break and can get at from where it is.** A shot breaks the first standing pixel it meets or bounces off it. Flak, cannons and trackers aim only where the first thing in the way is something they out-pierce (with nothing such, they shoot all the same and the shots bounce); a ship that lines a gun up lines up from a side that's open to it; and a ship picking its course counts its guns only from where they can do something, so ships that can't break the armour in front of them fly round to where they can.
- **An engine pushes its ship one way: the way it's turned.** Ahead as it comes. In the yard, [T] (or the turn button) turns the engine you paint to push to the right, astern or to the left, and the mirror puts its twin down pushing the mirrored way. So a thruster on a flank slides the ship and one on the prow brakes it, and you draw each on purpose. Its exhaust goes out the other way from its push and needs a straight way out of the ship, 3 wide, as an engine's always did: now that one way. With none it's buried, shows red and pushes nothing. Shoot off a thruster and the ship can't push that way any more.
- **A ship keeps its speed** until an engine pushes against it. One with no engine left drifts on, and the edge of the map stops it.
- **A ship faces one of four ways and turns a quarter at a time.** How long a quarter turn takes is how spread out its weight is (each pixel's weight, times one plus the square of how far it sits from the middle) over what turns it: 2 for each core, and for each **rotation engine** (GYRO, a new part) 3 for every pixel it sits from the middle, plus one. So a compact ship turns faster than a long one, and a rotation engine does more out at an end. The slowest turn is 3 seconds a quarter. What can't fly can't turn.
- **An engine that's pushing leaves exhaust:** a pixel in its side's dim colour, left in space where the engine's exhaust comes out, every third tick, for 20 ticks. A ship that's coasting leaves nothing, and no ship has a tail from its middle.
- **The plain pilot** flies a ship that isn't in a fight. Every tick it burns whichever engines bring its speed nearer to what it wants. It makes for a speed both ways at once (see above), turning only when its engines can't do a fair share of the job as it faces, and it begins each stop early enough, counting the time to turn an engine against its way.
- **A ship with no guns flies as its plan says** (2026-11-19). It doesn't pick a course of its own, whatever is in sight: a miner works, and with a load goes to its node and stops; hit, it runs home and stops there; sent, it goes. (Every ship used to pick a course, weighed over four seconds. A ship with no guns that took longer than that to turn about and stop never began to: having taken up a speed to get clear of a gun it kept it to the edge of the map. Armed ships that turn as slowly still weigh four seconds.)
- **A ship with a gun picks its own course in a fight** (2026-11-16). Every 4 ticks it plays its own next four seconds forward some thirty ways (as its plan says, holding each of a spread of speeds, stepping aside for a moment, going round its target) and takes the one that costs it least in pixels. It counts shots already in the air that would cross it; enemy flak and turrets, which fire at where it will be if it keeps its speed, so changing speed gets it out from under them, and a turret has to swing round to it first; the lines enemy lances are on; its own guns in reach, and its lance crossing a pixel of its target; and how far it is from where its orders want it. That last isn't the pilot's to change: a ship with no gun to line up holds its range from its target and dodges round it, and any other has a 24 px leash round its spot. It doesn't back away from a fight it's losing, unless it's sent somewhere else: a ship on its way to where it was sent stops for nothing, fires at what comes in reach as it passes, dodges, and keeps its group's pace.
- **The ships drawn for it:** ARROW (one engine astern and two rotation engines: a quarter turn in 2 ticks), SKATER (an engine astern and one on each flank turned to push across: it slides without turning), BRAKER (an engine astern and one on its prow turned to push astern: it stops without turning), LANCET (a lance, one engine, two rotation engines), GUNBOAT (a cannon on the smallest hull that feeds it, with two rotation engines), the starter deck's fighter and frigate as they were drawn before any of this (every engine pushing ahead, and no rotation engines: the frigate takes 3 seconds a quarter turn), SIEGE's SENTINEL (a tracker), and TURTLE (a hull in a shell of plate, open only astern past its engines). Nine fights.

## The match (Match round 2)

A 1v1 against the AI on a mirrored 1400x1050 map: kill its king. From the yard, **MATCH** asks whether you play your team of nine as your deck or the starter deck, and which ready-made team the AI plays (click its button for the next): the starter deck, as the AI always played it, or one of five teams with a strategy each (below). Your first seven slots start out as the starter deck, so there's a working deck to redraw from the first minute. Each side starts with its king and a 190 px bank, nothing else. [MATCH.md](MATCH.md) has the plan, the numbers and what each round is testing.

- **A beaten king is slow.** After the first minute, a king whose side has under 40 px of ships and factories standing (everything of its team but the king itself) flies at a tenth of its speed: 3.75 px/s, not 37.5 (7.5 until 2026-11-17, when the slowest ship went from 11 px/s to 7.5). That is slower than the slowest ship a drawing can make, so anything with an engine catches it. It's back to full speed the moment its side is back to 40 px: a seed in flight and a factory still growing count, the bank doesn't. The field says so for either king, a slowed king's engines go dark, and the two lines under the small map show SLOW; hover a king for how much its side has standing. At full speed a king is as fast as any ship a drawing can make, and faster on a diagonal (the starter deck's fighters make 37 px/s), so one with nothing left to lose could otherwise run until the 40-minute draw.
- **Orders are the AI's (2026-11-14).** A drawing carries no order, and the yard has no order buttons. What a ship does follows from its drawing: with engines and a scoop it mines, with engines and no scoop it fights, with no engines it stays where it is. In your matches the ships that fight take the stance (GUARD or ATTACK) and go where you send them, and that's all. HUNT, SAB, GUARD and ESCORT are still what a side's AI tells its cards, one card at a time, and a command to give a card its own order is dropped when it comes from a person's side, yours or another player's. The AI orders everything of its that flies without a scoop, so its unarmed ships go with its fleet. HUNT goes after the nearest enemy, wherever it is. SAB goes after the nearest enemy miner or factory (never a king), past whatever guards it, and hunts when there are none left. GUARD waits at a post round what built the ship and fights what comes within 160 px of there. ESCORT forms up in rings round the king; nothing uses it today. Henry: "they can become part of the opponent AI perhaps (and encapsulated to that scope - remember we want to have at least 3 levels in the future) but I don't believe they should be part of, for example, the PvP 1v1 match at all."
- **A design's line** in a save or the library is `NAME|X,Y|rows|KIND|BUILD`. Lines saved earlier carried an order second and sometimes a way of flying and a group size on the end. They still load, and those three are read past.
- **Groups are gone (2026-11-14).** A design used to carry a group size of 1, 3, 6 or 12, and its ships gathered at their posts in that number before leaving on HUNT or SAB. Henry: "I think we can get rid of group size along with manual flight patterns." Ships leave as they're built.
- **Posts.** A ship with nothing to do waits at a post of its own: a place in the rings round what built it, which is its factory, or the spot where your king summoned it. Guards fill the rings in the order they were built. The first ring is 16 px outside the builder, clear of what it prints next, and rings and posts are about 16 px apart, so an outpost's first eight guards stand all round it and the ninth starts a second ring. At its post a ship looks outward and sits still. It gives way only to what can't move aside for it (a factory, a ship being printed, a king) and to a ship already waiting that was built before it: then it waits at the nearest place clear of them all (of two as near, the one further from home), and goes back when the way is free. Whatever flies past goes round it, except a miner at work, which flies over. A guard that leaves to fight comes back to the post it had; the rings close up when one is lost. Ships your king summons within 24 px of posts already kept join those rings.
- **Designs are built or seeded** (yard: BUILT / SEEDED). A seeded design's core stays at the grid's centre, where its seed lands, and it grows outward from there. It needs a rock at least as big as itself, or it stays unfinished. A seeded design may have engines and guns: it grows out of its rock, then flies off when it's whole.
- **Factory pixels** (Industry, 2 power each) print a design's build order (yard: START, REPEAT and ADD, by deck slot): the START row once, in order, then the REPEAT row round and round, sixteen in each. A click on a row's name lights it, ADD puts a design on the end of the lit row, and a right-click takes an entry off. Each prints 1 pixel about every 27 s, so the outpost's 24 print 0.9 px/s, and each lets the factory build ships up to 10 px bigger. It builds the next thing on its list that it's big enough for, pays as it prints, and waits when the bank is empty; printers sharing a bank take turns, a pixel each. Nothing grows, prints or arrives by hyperspace into an enemy's pixels: it waits or grows round. Carriers (built ships with factory pixels) print the same way.
- **Pixels come from rocks.** Every scoop bites a pixel out of a rock every 10 s, so the starter miner's three bite one every 3.3 s and six would bite twice as fast (since 2026-11-17; before that nothing past two scoops bit any faster). A ship with scoops and a tractor of its own empties itself as it works and never hauls. Nothing takes its load the moment it's home (2026-11-16): a **tractor** pulls it off, a pixel every 8 s for each tractor pixel, so eight pull a pixel a second. A full miner flies to the tractor where it will be empty soonest (the flight, and the loads already waiting there), stops inside its reach and waits. One that works inside a tractor's reach is emptied as it works and never leaves the rock. Your king has eight tractor pixels; the starter outpost has four and the shipyard eight; a factory drawn without any takes nothing in. A dotted pink line shows each pull, one pink dot going round the tractor ship under the pointer or selected shows how far it reaches (a lap in 4 s, then 3 s of rest), and when four or more of your miners are standing waiting, a small warning triangle with a pink square and their number shows at the top of the field.
- **Rock first (2026-11-19).** A miner goes for rock while there's any within 350 px of it (a quarter of the map's width, as the crow flies), and leaves loose pixels lying; its scoops take whatever comes within their 8 px. With no rock that near it goes for the nearest loose pixel that's nearer than the nearest rock, and with no rock left on the map it takes loose pixels wherever they lie. Wreckage beside rock is a vacuum's work. (Until then a loose pixel within 30 px came before any rock, and a miner in a field of wreckage walked off along it.)
- **Collectors.** A ship that flies with a tractor and no scoop follows your miners: it stands where its tractor covers the most of them that no other tractor has room for (eight tractor pixels keep three miners emptied), and no further from your network than still links, so what it pulls can be spent. Send it by hand and it goes where it's sent; G lets it go back. The starter deck's COLLECTOR is 27 px with eight tractor pixels. A miner at work flies straight to its rock pixel and straight home with a full load: it keeps apart from nobody and nobody keeps apart from it, so miners heap up on a rock face and fly through each other. Each bite sparks on the rock, and a scoop turns the colour of loose pixels for each scoopful aboard, so you can see a miner working and how full it is. Your king also makes one pixel every 4 s by itself, so a side that has lost every miner can still print one.
- **The network:** your king and anything with factory pixels or tractor pixels are nodes. Nodes store pixels and link (dotted lines) when within reach of each other, and linked nodes share a bank. What a tractor pulls is stored in its own ship, and what nodes that fly hold is spent first. A node that flies out of link by itself hasn't split anything. Cut a link and the bank splits by where the pixels sit; a destroyed node spills what it held. A note says so when your network breaks in two: two nodes parting, or your king cut off by a node you lost. Your king flying out of reach by itself breaks nothing, and says nothing. BANK is what your king's network holds; a `+N` beside it is what nodes cut off from your king hold. Fly near them, or plant a link, to spend it.
- **A map's rocks are laid out one of four ways,** as the match's seed falls (2026-11-17): scattered all over with a giant in the middle, as every map used to be; or strung along one big figure, with open space everywhere else: an S across the map, a ring round an empty middle, or two triangles with an open way between them. Every layout is the same turned half round, has rock within reach of each king's first factory, and holds about as much rock. The match log says which a match was played on.
- **Rocks have no owners.** Anyone can mine any rock, and a seed can eat any rock. The planted factory is the claim. A rock is every rock pixel that touches, edge to edge or corner to corner, so a factory planted on the very edge of one has the whole of it to eat.
- **Factories stay put, keep apart and are solid.** No factory grows into another, yours or the enemy's: a seed sent where one already stands comes down beside it and grows what it has room for (the planting bar warns you first). Whatever is planted on a rock is solid to every enemy ship, whatever its size: a king flown at an enemy factory stops against its plate. Your own ships fly over your own factories, and your miners work the rock under them and under any ships parked there. A bigger ship does pass over a drifting hulk with no engines, without pushing it. Rocks themselves stop nothing.
- **The AI** plays by the same commands you do. It plants a factory as soon as its bank holds the price (and never lets you have more factories than it has for long, nor lets its bank sit full: half a minute of that plants another), keeps printing miners while the rock in reach of its factories and king has room for more (up to 30 of its own), prints a collector before another miner when its tractors can't pull as fast as two thirds of its miners bite, masses its warships on GUARD, and sends them out as a wave (the slow designs first, so they arrive together) when it's the stronger side or has rested long enough. A raider within 200 px of its king, anything it has planted or one of its miners turns the guards out, and they stay out until every raider is 60 px further off than that. Your king counts as a raider from the moment it crosses to the AI's half of the map. Its king prints what counters your fleet: something big against small flak ships, a flak swarm against small lance ships, lances against big ships (and against your king, if it comes alone).
  - **When it arms.** A raid that outweighs every warship it has comes before miners. While your fleet is a quarter bigger than its own, guns come before what it's saving for a factory. Otherwise it prints miners first and guns with what's left over.
  - **Where its king stands.** While a raid is on, its king moves beside the factory nearest its start, so the factory's flak and its own cover each other (unless moving would cut one of its factories off from its bank). It stays there against anything it and the armed friends round it outweigh. Outweighed, it falls back on the nearest of its factories with no enemy gun near, and with none it backs straight away.
  - **Where it plants under a raid.** Against a raid it outweighs, as on any day. Against one that outweighs it, never on a rock the raiders are at, and only where the new factory joins its network.
- **The AI's teams (2026-11-14; given the new parts 2026-11-15).** The match menu's THE AI PLAYS button picks which ready-made team the AI plays; a click goes on to the next. Each is also a team on the library's shelf, to load and play yourself or change. The starter deck is played as above. The other five come with a strategy that only the AI uses: which factories it plants and from when, how many miners it keeps, what its king prints, when its first wave may go and how many pixels of warships a wave needs, whether it goes whatever you have (bold) or waits for the edge as above, and which cards raid. Whatever the strategy, it answers raids as above. The teams are in `src/pg/teams.rs`.
  - **SWARM**, a rush: fighters, lancers, miners, the NEST (a 41 px factory of ten factory pixels, two tractor pixels and four flak, which builds up to 100 px), the outpost and the AP LANCER (the lancer with a piercer between its lances, so it breaks heavy plate; its outposts print them for the boom). Nests at once, at 1:40 and at 3:20, then outposts; one miner a factory and one more; its king prints fighters and lancers in turn and saves nothing. Its first wave goes at 2:30 with 54 px or more, whatever you have, and again whenever it has 54 px.
  - **LANCE LINE**, a timing attack: fighters, the CORVETTE (29 px, three lances behind a nose of heavy plate), the CRUISER (132 px, twelve lances in five columns behind a cap of heavy plate, and a tracker), miners, the outpost and the DOCK (a 269 px factory of 66 factory pixels and six tractor pixels, which builds up to 660 px). Two outposts, a dock, then outposts; two miners a factory and one more; its king prints corvettes. Its first wave goes at 7:00 with 400 px or more, whatever you have.
  - **BIG GUNS**, a boom: fighters, the frigate, miners, the outpost, the shipyard, the BATTLESHIP (195 px and two cores: three cannons, a tracker, eight lances and twelve flak behind a cap of heavy plate, at a frigate's speed) and the capital. Four outposts, then a shipyard; two miners a factory and one more; its outposts build mostly miners and its king prints frigates and fighters for home. Its first wave waits for 12:00, 900 px and the edge.
  - **RAIDERS**, harassment: the WASP (12 px, a three-pixel repeater, the fastest thing that flies), the ARCHER, the RAKER, miners and the outpost. Outposts only, up to five. Its wasps go after your miners and factories from the moment each is built, and stay at it. Its first wave of the rest waits for 5:00, 150 px and the edge.

### Against a person (the network spike)

Two games play one match, each player with their own team of nine, on one machine or two. It's a spike: enough to find out how a match feels with lag, and whether two machines stay in step. There's an address to type and nothing else: no list of games, no second go, no pause.

- **To play:** on the match menu one player picks **HOST A MATCH** and the other **JOIN A MATCH**, types the host's address (it starts as 127.0.0.1, which is this machine) and presses Enter. The host plays blue, the joiner red. From a command line it's `--host [PORT]` and `--join ADDR[:PORT]`; `--host local` hears only this machine, and `--windowed` puts a game in a window, so two fit on one screen. The port is 7777, TCP. To host for another machine that port has to be open to it, and Windows asks about its firewall the first time.
- **How it works:** each machine runs the whole match, and all they send each other is commands. A command is applied a few ticks after it's given (the lag), so the other machine has it in time, and a tick is played only when a machine holds both sides' commands for it. A machine still short of the other's waits, and says WAITING FOR THE OTHER PLAYER.
- **The lag dial** (L, or LAG at the top of the field): how many ticks your own commands wait. A tick is 67 ms. A network match starts at 2 and goes 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8; with less than the connection needs, the match stutters. Against the AI the dial is there too, from 0: that's how a network match of that lag would feel, with no network.
- **The check:** once a second both machines hash the whole match and compare. IN STEP and a time, at the top of the field, is the last second they agreed on. If they ever differ it says OUT OF STEP and since when. From there the two screens show different games: that's a fault in the game, and the log has the tick.
- **What a network match can't do:** pause, run fast, or stop while the rules or the quit question are up. LEAVE ends it for both players. If the other player goes, it ends with no result.

**The starting library.** With no save file, your first eight slots are the starter deck, and slot 9 is empty: FIGHTER, LANCER, FRIGATE, MINER, OUTPOST, SHIPYARD, CAPITAL, COLLECTOR (the collector since 2026-11-16). They are Henry's drawings of 2026-11-10: every card spends all the power its hull makes, so none carries pixels that do nothing (`--deck` prints the sheet). The miner was redrawn once more that day, after eighteen drawings were played against each other. On 2026-11-11 a lance, a factory pixel and a scoop each came to use more power (and a scoop to weigh 2), and the five cards that left short were given the hull they need, shapes and sizes kept: the lancer is 9 px, the miner 9 px with two engines, the outpost has 28 factory pixels and the shipyard 118. Those five are stand-ins, drawn by rule and not by play. A save still holding an untouched card from before gets today's when it loads. Delete `proving_ground_designs.txt` to get it back. The fleet the rounds were judged on (fighter, lancer, frigate, destroyer, capital, gunminer, titan) lives on in the code with its own nine tests. ## Controls

| Input | Does |
|---|---|
| WASD / arrows | Fly your king. If it dies, you lose. |
| 1–5 or click a card | Print it. Seed cards then need a rock: click one on the map, minimap or tactical view. |
| Right-click / Esc | Cancel planting |
| Tab / TAC | Tactical view: the whole map at exactly 1/4 scale |
| Backspace / UNDO | Take the last card off the print queue (printed pixels come back) |
| Right-click a queued card | Repeat it every time it finishes (blue frame); seeds can't repeat |
| P / PAUSE, F1 / HELP, F2 | Pause, rules, scanlines |

## Rules in one breath

- Pixels are money. Every design costs its pixel count, paid one pixel per frame as it prints.
- Collectors chew pixels off rocks and wrecks and haul them to the nearest factory.
- A seed flies to a rock and eats it into a base, pixel by pixel.
- A base keeps its pinned build order alive, rebuilding whatever dies:
  - **Outpost:** 2 collectors, 1 escort, and 1 outpost seed. The seed means outposts spread.
  - **Forward base:** 2 escorts and 3 raiders.
- Raiders wait at their factory until there are 3, then the pack hunts.
- Every colour is a part: yellow shoots, blue-white pushes, orange scoops, grey takes two hits, and a ship dies when its white core is gone.
- Shots knock pixels loose for anyone to collect.

## Tools

- `--sim N`: plays N AI-vs-AI matches and N AI-vs-idle-king matches headless, then prints the stats.
- `--trace SEED [--both]`: prints one match's state every 20 seconds.
- `--export-screenshots DIR`: 2x PNGs of the main screens.

Playtests append to `nomindwing_playtest.log` (gitignored): every card played, every base planted or lost, and a snapshot every 30 seconds.

## Tuning notes (2026-11-07)

- **Map:** 960x720. Every rock has a mirror twin through the centre. An earlier centre rock sat 0.35 px closer to one king, and that king won 5 of 6 AI matches.
- **Aiming:** gunners aim at a random surviving pixel. Aiming at the centre drilled a straight tunnel through a king that later shots flew through without touching anything.
- **Pacing:** the AI builds economy first, plants its forward base at about 1:50, prints no raiders before 1:40, and doesn't send its king before 3:20. An idle king falls at about 2:10. AI-vs-AI matches end in 3½–6½ minutes, roughly 50/50.
